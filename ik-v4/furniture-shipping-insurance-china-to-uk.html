Why carrier liability is limited

Furniture shipping insurance from China to the UK should be considered before collection. A freight booking does not promise reimbursement of the full invoice if furniture arrives damaged. Carrier liability concerns the carrier's responsibility under the transport contract and applicable law. Cargo insurance is a separate contract protecting an insured interest in the goods.

The Hague-Visby Rules contain carrier duties, defences and limits. Where they apply, compensation can be limited by package or weight rather than simply matching the purchase price. Article IV rule 5 of the Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1971 schedule sets out the framework, including how declared value and package descriptions matter. Which regime applies depends on the carriage; ask the forwarder or a legal adviser about your bill of lading. A liability limit is not an insurance valuation.

This distinction matters for furniture. A high-value finished cabinet can be relatively light, while one damaged panel can prevent a complete room being installed. Keep a packing list identifying the contents of each numbered carton. Ask how packages will be described on the transport document before it is issued.

Cargo insurance versus carrier liability

With cargo insurance, you claim under the policy for an insured loss, subject to its conditions, evidence requirements and any excess. A carrier claim depends on responsibility under the relevant carriage terms. Neither route guarantees payment. Ask who receives the claim, who is insured and which currency and shipment value appear on the certificate.

Agree cover before movement starts. Describe factory collection, consolidation, planned warehouse stages, the sea journey and final delivery. Ask where insurance begins and ends, and whether loading, unloading and storage fit the insured journey. A description such as door to door is not enough: have the actual locations and stages confirmed.

Institute Cargo Clauses A, B and C

Institute Cargo Clauses (A) use a broad physical loss or damage approach, subject to exclusions. Institute Cargo Clauses (B) and Institute Cargo Clauses (C) cover specified events; B generally has a wider list than C. The letters describe different wordings, not a guarantee that a particular scratch, wet carton or broken tabletop will be paid for.

Ask for the clause edition, schedule and endorsements. Discuss breakage, surface damage, moisture and handling for your furniture. Policies vary; read the wording. The Lloyd's cargo claims and recoveries manual explains the clause families and exclusions. Use the issued policy to establish what applies to your shipment.

Agree the insured value basis

Cargo insurance is commonly arranged on CIF + 10%: the cost of the goods, insurance and freight, plus a further ten per cent. This is a valuation basis to discuss with the insurer, not a promised settlement or a price for cover. CargoCover's insurance FAQs explain the components and the importance of an accurate declared value.

Provide the commercial invoice and freight information, then ask which associated costs belong in the calculation. Agree the currency and treatment of separate supplier invoices. If quantities or goods change, update the declaration before dispatch. Keep a schedule connecting the insured shipment to purchase orders and cartons, especially when several factories contribute to the same load.

Exclusions to discuss before shipping

Typical exclusions include insufficient packing, inherent vice and delay. Inherent vice concerns loss arising from the goods' own nature or condition. Policies vary; read the wording, including qualifications and additional conditions. Broad cover does not replace suitable packaging or make a missed installation date an insured loss.

Describe fragile materials, finishes and known condition issues honestly. Ask what packing evidence is needed and whether an inspection or survey is required before collection. Request written clarification where a term is unclear. Keep the response with the certificate so the receiving team can find it alongside the claims contact details.

Package furniture around its weak points

Agree a packing specification with the factory before production finishes. Protect corners, edges, polished surfaces and projecting handles. Separate components so metal fixings cannot rub against finished faces. Use suitable cushioning and rigid support for glass, stone and long panels, with the method agreed for the item's weight and handling needs.

  • Secure hardware in labelled bags away from finished furniture surfaces.
  • Support heavy parts without concentrating weight on thin boards or decorative trim.
  • Check protective wrapping and adhesives for compatibility with the finish.
  • Agree moisture precautions for the actual materials; do not seal damp goods inside wrapping.
  • Mark carton numbers, contents, orientation and handling needs clearly.
  • Photograph the item, internal protection, closed carton and labels before collection.

Ask the inspector to record packing against the specification, rather than simply counting sealed cartons. Our sourcing, inspection and visits service can help coordinate checks. Compare LCL and FCL shipping when planning handling: either option needs furniture prepared for transport.

What to do on delivery

Arrange someone who knows the order to receive it, with the packing list and a camera available. Inspect visible condition and count packages before signing. Note damage and shortages on the delivery note before signing, using carton numbers and specific descriptions. If cartons cannot be opened then, record the actual inspection limitations accurately; do not certify unseen contents as checked.

Photograph the load where possible, all sides of damaged cartons, labels and the goods as they are unpacked. Keep wide views showing context as well as close-ups. Keep packaging and damaged parts. Separate affected items safely and prevent further damage where practical, but ask for instructions before repairs, disposal or return.

Notify the insurer or claims contact and relevant carrier promptly in writing. Follow the certificate's instructions and ask about deadlines immediately, including concealed damage. Send initial facts even if documents are still being gathered. Keep your notification, delivery note and acknowledgement together.

Claim evidence checklist

  • Commercial invoice: identifies the supplier, goods and invoiced values.
  • Packing list: connects each affected item to its carton and shipment.
  • Bill of lading: records the sea carriage and package description.
  • Inspection photos: show condition and packing before shipment, plus arrival and unpacking images.
  • Delivery note: records visible damage, shortages and inspection limitations at handover.
  • Survey report: obtain one if requested; ask the claims handler who should appoint the surveyor.

Also keep the insurance certificate, written notifications and a factual timeline. Use consistent item references and quantities across documents. Ask before commissioning repairs or accepting a replacement: the handler may need an opportunity to inspect the furniture and consider recovery options.

Frequently asked questions

Is furniture cargo insurance automatic?

No. IK ENG arranges cargo insurance on request. Confirm the policy, insured value and journey in writing before collection.

Does broad cargo cover pay for every kind of damage?

No. Institute Cargo Clauses (A) remain subject to exclusions and policy conditions. Policies vary; read the wording and ask about your furniture and packing.

What should I do if furniture arrives damaged?

Inspect, note visible damage on the delivery note before signing, take photos, keep packaging and notify the claims contact and carrier promptly in writing.
