Furniture shipping insurance China to UK

Cargo insurance can protect your furniture against insured transit damage. Agree the cover before collection, then preserve the evidence from packing through delivery.

Why the carrier may not pay the full furniture value

Furniture shipping insurance from China to the UK deserves attention before collection, especially for fragile finishes, stone tops and upholstered pieces. A freight booking does not automatically insure your purchase price. Carrier liability concerns what a carrier is legally responsible for under the transport contract and applicable rules; cargo insurance is separate protection purchased for the goods.

The Hague-Visby Rules set duties, defences and limits rather than promising full replacement cost. The current Schedule to the Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1971, Article IV rule 5, provides a limit of 666.67 units of account per package or unit, or 2 units of account per kilogramme of gross weight lost or damaged, whichever is higher, subject to the rule's qualifications. These units are Special Drawing Rights, not pounds. The bill of lading's package description can matter.

Do not apply that calculation automatically to every China-to-UK shipment. Which rules govern a particular movement depends on the contract and applicable law. A limit is also not a guaranteed payment: responsibility, defences and evidence still matter. A light but valuable cabinet may have an invoice value quite different from a weight-based liability calculation. Ask for the transport terms and insurance proposal before authorising collection.

Cargo insurance versus carrier liability

A cargo policy responds to insured loss under its own wording. You claim under the policy rather than assuming the carrier will reimburse everything. The insurer may then pursue recovery from a responsible party. You still need to preserve evidence and follow the policy's notification and claims instructions; buying cover does not remove those obligations.

Check who is named as the insured, which goods are scheduled and where cover starts and ends. Confirm collection, intermediate handling, any planned storage and final delivery in writing. Ask about unloading at site and whether installation is outside the insured journey. A vague promise of door-to-door shipping is not a substitute for a defined insurance period.

Shipping terms and insurance answer different questions. Read our DDP guide when comparing supplier arrangements, but confirm insurance separately even where delivery is included. Ask who would submit a claim, who receives any settlement and what excess applies.

Institute Cargo Clauses A, B and C in plain terms

Proposals may name Institute Cargo Clauses (A), Institute Cargo Clauses (B) or Institute Cargo Clauses (C). The letter describes a standard starting point; the dated wording, schedule and endorsements determine your actual contract. Ask for those documents rather than relying on a short sales description.

  • Institute Cargo Clauses (A): broad physical loss or damage cover, subject to exclusions. Often described as all risks, but that does not mean every loss is insured.
  • Institute Cargo Clauses (B): cover based on specified events, broader than C but narrower than A. Check whether the cause of the furniture damage falls within the listed events.
  • Institute Cargo Clauses (C): a narrower set of specified events. It should not be treated as general protection against routine handling damage.

The Lloyd's cargo claims and recoveries manual includes the clause texts. For your order, ask the insurance provider to explain scratches, breakage and water damage against the proposed wording. Do not assume a label settles whether a particular incident is covered.

Agree the insured value and exclusions

The insured value basis is commonly CIF + 10%: the goods' cost, insurance and freight, plus an additional ten per cent. That is a common valuation basis, not a universal requirement or a promise of an extra payment. Agree the calculation with the provider, record the currency and check which invoice and freight amounts it uses. Keep the calculation with the certificate.

Describe the goods accurately, including fragile components, mixed materials and any used items. If an order changes before dispatch, update the schedule and value rather than assuming the original certificate follows it. Ask how partial damage, repair and replacement are assessed, including what evidence is needed where a replacement part is available.

Typical exclusions include insufficient packing, inherent vice and delay; policies vary, so read the wording. Inherent vice means a loss arising from the goods' own nature or condition rather than an external insured event. Moisture-sensitive materials and finishes need particular care, but the cause and wording decide a claim. Do not assume a delayed project, missed opening date or consequential business loss is insured.

Packaging furniture to reduce damage

Agree a packing specification before production finishes. Protection should suit the item and the full journey, including lifting and intermediate handling. Compare LCL and FCL shipping when planning the load; neither service removes the need for suitable packaging.

  • Protect corners, edges and finished faces without placing abrasive material against polished surfaces.
  • Secure doors and drawers using protection that will not mark the finish. Label separately packed fittings so they can be matched at delivery.
  • Separate glass, stone and other fragile parts from components that could strike them. Agree supports and rigid protection appropriate to the piece.
  • Prevent movement inside cartons or crates. Show handlers safe lifting points and do not rely on fragile stickers alone.
  • Discuss moisture protection with the factory and pack only when the product and packing are suitable for dispatch. Wrapping should not trap avoidable moisture.

Ask for photographs of the bare product, internal protection and closed package with its carton mark visible. Record packed dimensions and weights on the packing list. Our inspection and factory visits service can help check agreed requirements before release. Inspection reduces uncertainty; it does not replace insurance or guarantee that damage cannot occur later.

What to do when the furniture arrives

Plan a receiving team and a dry space before the delivery appointment. Have the packing list available and compare carton marks and quantities as the load arrives. Inspect accessible packages and goods safely. Note visible damage, wet cartons, crushed corners or shortages on the delivery note before signing, and ask for a copy showing those observations.

Take photographs before moving damaged packages: capture the whole load, package labels, the affected carton and close-ups of the damage. Photograph each layer as you unpack. Keep packaging, supports and broken parts because they may help establish how damage happened. If the driver cannot wait for full unpacking, record that inspection was limited and notify any later findings promptly; do not assume a general reservation alone protects a claim.

Notify the insurance contact and relevant transport contact promptly in writing, following their instructions and deadlines. Include shipment references and an initial description, even if the final repair estimate is not ready. Take reasonable steps to prevent further damage where safe, but ask before disposing of goods or arranging substantial repairs. Request survey instructions if damage is significant.

Claim evidence checklist

  • Commercial invoice: goods descriptions, quantities, values and currency.
  • Packing list: carton marks, contents, weights and package counts.
  • Bill of lading: transport reference and package description.
  • Inspection photos: pre-dispatch condition, packing and arrival damage.
  • Delivery note: dated receipt with damage or shortage recorded before signing.
  • Survey report: where requested or arranged, with findings and affected items identified.

Also retain the policy certificate, correspondence and any requested repair quotation. Keep a simple timeline of collection, delivery, discovery and notification. Separate damaged items from sound items on the schedule so the insurer can follow the evidence without guessing which photograph belongs to which invoice line.

Frequently asked questions

Is furniture cargo insurance included automatically?

IK ENG arranges cargo insurance on request. Confirm the policy, insured value, exclusions and start of cover in writing before the goods move.

Does all risks cover every kind of furniture damage?

No. Institute Cargo Clauses (A) remain subject to exclusions and policy conditions. Ask the provider to explain how the proposed wording applies to your goods.

What should I keep if furniture arrives damaged?

Keep the packaging, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, inspection photos, delivery note and any survey report. Notify the relevant contacts promptly in writing.
