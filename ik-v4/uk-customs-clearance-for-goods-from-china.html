UK customs clearance for goods from China

Prepare a consistent document pack before your goods leave China, then agree the declaration and payment arrangements with your customs broker. Start with the invoice, packing list, transport document and accurate goods descriptions. The exact evidence depends on the goods, route and customs procedure.

Build the document pack before collection

Use this as a preparation checklist, then ask the broker to confirm the shipment-specific requirements. GOV.UK: checks before appointing a customs representative lists EORI, invoices or contracts, goods descriptions and relevant licences or certificates among the information to provide.

  • Commercial invoice: obtain clear buyer and seller details, product descriptions, quantities, prices, currency and agreed trading terms. Avoid vague descriptions such as “parts” or “furniture” without material and use.
  • Packing list: ask the supplier to map each carton or crate to the invoice items, with quantities, weights and packed dimensions. Reconcile totals across suppliers.
  • Bill of lading or air waybill: obtain the transport document appropriate to the booking. Check names, package count and shipment references with the forwarder.
  • Commodity code: give the broker sufficient detail about materials, function and construction to check classification.
  • Origin evidence: ask whether a certificate of origin or other proof is relevant to the goods or treatment claimed. Do not order a generic certificate without checking.

For declarations selected for documentary checks, HMRC asks for supporting evidence including value documents, a packing list and any licences or certificates. See GOV.UK: supporting documents for customs checks. GOV.UK: proof of origin explains that proof used for preferential origin depends on the relevant trade agreement. Do not assume Chinese dispatch alone establishes origin or a reduced duty entitlement; check GOV.UK: rules of origin.

Check commodity codes and customs value

The import declaration needs the commodity code and goods value. The code affects duty and licence requirements; see GOV.UK: importing goods into the UK. Have the broker check the description against the actual goods rather than copying a supplier code unchecked. For kitchen orders, read HS codes for kitchen cabinets. For furniture tax planning, use the duty and VAT guide.

How duty and import VAT are paid

Agree the payment method before the declaration. HMRC offers immediate payment, CDS cash accounting and duty deferment arrangements. You can authorise a representative to use your relevant account. GOV.UK: paying import duties and VAT explains the options. Ask whether the broker uses your account or an agreed account of its own, and how any advance funding and fees are shown.

Postponed VAT accounting

A UK VAT-registered business may account for import VAT on its VAT Return instead of paying upfront. Normal input-tax recovery rules still apply. For business goods, HMRC requires the right to dispose of them and your VAT registration number on the declaration. Give your representative written instructions before the declaration; see GOV.UK: postponed VAT accounting.

PVA is an import VAT arrangement. Keep any Customs Duty funding separate, and ask your accountant to reconcile your postponed import VAT statement. Do not assume it has been selected just because you gave someone your VAT number.

What the customs broker does

The broker prepares and submits the declaration using your information and agreed instructions, and deals with customs queries. Representation terms must be confirmed in writing, specifying direct or indirect representation; see GOV.UK: appointing a customs representative. You still need to review the information supplied and keep the shipment records. Our forwarder, agent and broker guide explains where the roles overlap.

IK ENG arranges UK clearance through an authorised UK customs broker. You can be the importer, or request DDP. Confirm the importer’s identity and EORI requirements before collection.

Common causes of delays to resolve early

  • Invoice descriptions that do not identify the goods or materials.
  • Packing-list quantities or package totals that do not reconcile.
  • Missing importer information or unclear representation instructions.
  • Commodity code or value questions that need supplier evidence.
  • Required certificates not available when the declaration is prepared.
  • Duty funding or written PVA instructions not agreed in advance.

This is a practical prevention checklist, not a guarantee of clearance. Send drafts to your broker early and name someone who can answer supplier questions. For several suppliers, keep each invoice traceable through consolidation. See shipping Alibaba orders or the furniture import guide for the wider buying process.

Frequently asked questions

What documents should I send my customs broker?

Start with the commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, importer details and goods descriptions. Ask the broker which commodity codes, origin evidence or certificates are needed for your goods.

Can postponed VAT accounting pay my Customs Duty?

No. Postponed VAT accounting deals with import VAT on your VAT Return. Agree a separate payment arrangement for any Customs Duty due.

Does appointing a broker remove my responsibilities?

No. HMRC says you remain responsible for due diligence for your customs declarations when appointing someone to deal with customs.

Does every shipment need a certificate of origin?

Do not assume a certificate is always required. Ask the broker what origin evidence applies to your goods and any treatment being claimed.
