How it works for wardrobe and joinery buyers

  1. Measure the finished room. Record widths, heights, depths, slopes, uneven walls, skirting and services. Have your fitter review the site information and delivery route.
  2. Plan the internal layout. List hanging, shelves, drawers and door types. Check door travel and access against beds, sockets and radiators before deciding the exterior appearance.
  3. Approve the drawing set. Agree plans, elevations, sections, fillers, unit references, hardware and finish samples. Keep a revision number and a named approver for changes.
  4. Inspect against approved references. Compare finished units with the drawings and samples, including dimensions, internal fittings, door and drawer movement, hardware and packing.
  5. Label for fitting. Agree property, room and unit references on cartons. Give the approved drawings to your fitter and confirm site delivery and separate installation arrangements.

Measurements come before final pricing

Joinery is priced from the unit schedule rather than a flat rate per metre. A reference image cannot define internal compartments, wall tolerances or door clearances. Recheck dimensions after plastering and final floor finishes, and resolve filler and scribe details with the fitter before signing off manufacture. Inspection checks the approved specification; it cannot correct an inaccurate site survey.

Wardrobe and joinery buyers FAQs

What measurements are needed for fitted wardrobes?

Record width, height and depth in several places, along with slopes, uneven walls, skirting, sockets and other obstructions. Recheck after final wall and floor finishes and have your fitter review the drawings before approval.

Can you quote wardrobes from a photograph?

Reference images help explain the style, but final pricing needs room measurements and an approved unit schedule. Record hanging space, shelving, drawers, door type, hardware and finishes rather than relying on a style name.

Is fitted wardrobe installation included?

Installation, site templating, wall preparation, electrical work and design fees are agreed separately and shown as their own quotation lines. Give your fitter the drawings before placing the order.

What is checked before bespoke joinery ships?

The inspection scope uses the approved drawings, specification and sample references. Checks cover dimensions, finishes, internal layout, fittings, door and drawer movement and packing. Inspection does not replace accurate measuring.
