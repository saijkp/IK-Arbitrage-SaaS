Student accommodation furniture specification checklist

A practical checklist for PBSA operators and student HMO landlords, from bedroom and communal schedules to model-room approval, packing and replacement stock.

Plan your student accommodation order

Build a specification around room types

A student accommodation furniture specification should let the operator, supplier, installer and receiving team identify the same product without interpretation. Start with measured layouts and a room register, then define a schedule for each bedroom type, studio and shared area. PBSA operators may have many repeated rooms; student HMO landlords may have several different layouts within one house.

Give every item a product code and revision. Record dimensions, material, finish, hardware, quantity, assembly method and the evidence required before approval. Separate bedroom furniture from kitchens, communal rooms and installation. The schedule should make omissions visible, including fixings, cable access, mattress protectors or wall supports where these are part of your chosen scope.

Example bedroom schedule: a template to adapt

The following table is a template, not a completed specification or a price list. Quantities are example allowances per bedroom; adapt them to measured layouts, occupancy and your operating standard. Confirm whether lighting and pin boards are supplied with the furniture or through another contractor.

Check the whole room in plan and elevation. Allow doors and drawers to open, keep the chair usable at the desk and confirm the route for moving a mattress into the room. Record skirtings, sockets and service access. A nominal room area does not tell the factory whether a particular wardrobe will clear a sloping ceiling or a projecting pipe box.

Kitchens and communal areas need their own schedule

Build shared-area quantities from the number of users and the way the space operates. List dining tables and chairs, lounge seating, occasional tables, storage, bins and any study furniture separately. For cluster flats, identify which kitchen and communal package belongs to each cluster rather than dividing a building total across bedrooms.

For kitchens, specify cabinet layout, worktop finish, handles, hinges, drawer fittings and the interfaces with appliances and sinks. Assign responsibility for final measurements, service cut-outs and installation. Check table dimensions against circulation and cleaning access, and review whether furniture can be moved for maintenance without damaging floors.

A communal schedule can use the same columns as the bedroom template, with a quantity per kitchen, cluster or lounge. Add a location reference and a total quantity after the room register is checked. Avoid mixing per-room and whole-building quantities in one column; that makes duplication harder to spot.

Durability: describe construction and serviceability

Specify board and surface materials by name and thickness, along with the finish on both exposed and hidden faces where relevant. Describe edge banding, corner treatment and how edges around cut-outs are sealed. Ask for a sample showing a finished corner and joint. Do not accept a finish swatch as proof of the assembled cabinet's durability.

Review hinges, runners and handles for access, adjustment and replacement. Ask the supplier to identify the hardware actually included, and keep a replacement reference rather than allowing unspecified equivalent parts. For beds and desks, check joint design, stability and whether repeated assembly is practical during room refurbishments.

If chairs or storage use castors, confirm suitability for the actual floor finish, attachment method and availability of replacements. For worktops and desk tops, agree cleaning products, edge details and expected use. Request evidence for any claimed performance; do not insert a test rating into the specification unless the supplier can support that claim for the exact construction.

Fire safety: upholstery and mattresses

The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988, as amended, are a key starting point for upholstered furniture and mattresses. Review the exact items, materials and applicable requirements before purchase. GOV.UK guidance on the 2025 amendments explains changes including removal of the display-label requirement; do not rely on an old supplier checklist.

Ask for traceable product evidence and the applicable permanent labels for upholstered items. Mattresses have particular treatment under the regulations, so do not assume a sofa label or generic fabric report proves mattress compliance. Record the supplier, model, filling, cover and evidence reviewed. Require fresh review if the factory proposes a different cover or filling.

For PBSA, confirm the wider specification with the responsible fire-safety adviser and the building's fire risk assessment. GOV.UK sleeping accommodation guidance covers fire-risk considerations; the purchasing schedule alone cannot determine every building's requirements. Approve a sample or model room before bulk production

Set an approval stage for the assembled bedroom package, ideally in a representative room. Check comfort, desk usability, wardrobe access, drawer travel and cleaning underneath furniture. Include the mattress, lighting provision and wall-mounted items so the team assesses the room as residents will use it.

Keep a signed approval record with photographs, dimensions, finish references and a list of corrections. Distinguish an approved sample from a sample approved subject to changes. Close those changes on revised drawings before release. Retain the approved reference and require written agreement for substitutions throughout production.

For smaller orders, focus physical samples on the parts carrying the most uncertainty: the chair, mattress, board edges and hardware. See ordering samples from China. Confirm supplier minimums for each selected finish and item using our furniture MOQ guide; do not assume one room's sample order establishes bulk-order terms.

Room-by-room packing and labelling

Use a carton label format showing project, building, floor, room or cluster, item code, quantity and carton sequence. Match it to a packing list. Where a product has multiple cartons, mark them as a set so an installation team can tell whether all parts are present before opening packaging.

Agree whether the supplier packs by room, room type or product, and plan unloading accordingly. Keep fixings and assembly instructions identifiable, with a controlled spare-fixings pack. Protect finished edges and separate hardware from surfaces it could scratch. Check labels and quantities during inspection against the latest room register.

Plan delivery around the academic year

Work backwards from the operator's actual handover and resident arrival dates. Term dates vary between institutions and accommodation arrangements; obtain the project's dates rather than assuming a national changeover week. Include time for inspection, receiving, assembly, snagging and final room checks in your programme.

Confirm access windows, vehicle restrictions, unloading responsibility, lift availability and where each delivery can be received. Sequence occupied buildings carefully and name the person who can accept each load. Record what happens if a room is unavailable or site works delay access. Agree any warehousing and staged-delivery arrangements as part of the written scope.

Replacement stock and repeat orders

Plan replacement stock, sometimes called attic stock, by item and likely maintenance need. Decide allowances from your own operating history, room count, storage capacity and the consequences of a missing part. There is no single percentage that suits every building. Keep mattresses, chairs, hinges, handles and fixings as separate decisions.

Label stored items with the same product codes used in the schedule and retain finish references, drawings and assembly instructions. Assign someone to record issues and replenishment. Ask about future availability and minimums before standardising a range, and consider whether a replacement can be fitted without changing adjacent furniture.

Frequently asked questions

What belongs in a student bedroom furniture schedule?

Start with the bed, mattress, desk, chair, storage, shelving, pin board and lighting provision. Adapt quantities and dimensions to the room type and operating brief.

Should we approve a model room before bulk ordering?

Approve a representative assembled room where practical, record corrections and freeze drawings, finishes and product codes before releasing bulk production.

How much replacement stock should we hold?

Set allowances by item using room count, maintenance history, storage capacity and replacement availability. Agree responsibility for tracking and replenishment.
