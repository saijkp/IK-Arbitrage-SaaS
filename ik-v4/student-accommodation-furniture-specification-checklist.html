Student accommodation furniture specification checklist

Build a student accommodation furniture specification with bedroom and communal templates, durability checks, fire-safety evidence and delivery planning.

Plan student accommodation sourcing

Build one controlled furniture schedule

A student accommodation furniture specification should turn room plans into an order that a supplier, installer and site team can all follow. For purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA), separate each bedroom and cluster type. For a student HMO, start with a measured room list and shared-space plan. Record what stays, what is replaced and who signs off changes.

Give every item a code, quantity, dimensioned drawing, finish reference and approval status. Keep the schedule revision on quotations and orders. Avoid approving a bed by photograph while a separate drawing shows a different footprint. An approved model room should represent the same product construction and accessories that will arrive on site.

Bedroom layout and practical use

Check bed access, wardrobe door swings and the route between desk and entrance. Include sockets, skirting boards, radiators and window openings on the drawings. A desk that fits a wall can still leave too little chair space once the bed is installed. Review unusual rooms individually rather than applying the typical layout without checking.

Write down the cleaning routine and likely replacement tasks. Can the team move a bed safely to clean behind it? Can a drawer runner or lamp be changed without dismantling neighbouring furniture? Mark handed units clearly. If beds have storage, demonstrate access with the mattress and other furniture in place. Ask the operator to review the furnished room from a maintenance perspective.

Kitchen and communal schedule

Prepare a separate schedule per shared kitchen, cluster lounge, laundry or study area. Count the occupants served and plan seating, dining surfaces and storage around the layout. Agree appliance positions before finalising cabinets. Confirm what the building contractor supplies so furniture, worktops, appliances and installation are not counted twice or omitted.

Durability: construction you can inspect

Ask for the board or timber specification, finish sample and details of exposed edges. Check edge banding at corners, desk fronts and cleaning contact points. Look for gaps, rough edges and poorly finished cut-outs on the sample. Define acceptable workmanship with photographs instead of descriptions such as “commercial quality”. Do not invent a performance rating where no evidence exists.

For hinges and drawer runners, record the component reference, adjustment method and replacement availability. Check that handles are secure and accessible. Where chairs or storage use castors, match them to the actual floor and ask whether replacements can be fitted. Review feet and glides too: small missing parts can make an otherwise serviceable item difficult to use.

For worktops and desk surfaces, approve the finish and cleaning instructions. Check joins, exposed edges and appliance cut-outs with the contractor. Ask the supplier to explain limits on moisture, heat and cleaning products. If a performance test matters to your operator, specify the required evidence and exact product before ordering; a catalogue claim is not a verified test result.

Upholstery and mattress fire-safety evidence

Include a compliance review for upholstered chairs, sofas, mattresses and bed bases. The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 are the starting point, alongside GOV.UK guidance and amendment information. Check current requirements for the actual product and use rather than treating a fabric description as approval of the finished item.

Landlords must ensure the furnishings they supply are fire safe; see GOV.UK landlord safety responsibilities. Keep applicable test evidence, product identification and required labelling records with the schedule. Have the competent project adviser check the evidence for the complete upholstery or mattress construction and confirm which labels are required. Do not assume every product category has identical labelling obligations.

Read our HMO furniture fire-safety guide alongside that review. Broader building fire precautions need their own assessment; purchasing furniture does not settle them. Require written approval before substituting foam, fabric or other specified materials during production. A visually similar replacement can have different construction and supporting evidence.

Approve a sample or model room before bulk manufacture

Use sample ordering to agree what will be supplied and what decisions it must resolve. For repeat rooms, assemble a representative model room with the proposed bed, mattress, desk, chair and storage. Check fit, finish, access, comfort and cleaning with the operator and installer. Photograph details and record every change against the schedule revision.

Approve the final amended version before bulk manufacture. Keep a reference sample or agreed photographic record and document list. State who can authorise changes, how deviations are recorded and which items need another sample. Ask for inspection against the approved specification, including dimensions, hardware, quantities, finish and accessory packs.

Room-by-room packing and delivery windows

Use a label hierarchy: building, floor, cluster, room, item code and carton sequence. Where an item spans several cartons, show the total number and match fixings to the assembly. Keep a master packing list and room allocation sheet. Separate spare stock from installation stock so the installation team does not use it unintentionally.

Plan backward from your own access and resident arrival dates. Term dates vary between institutions, and refurbishment windows depend on departures, cleaning and other trades. Agree the installation sequence with the site team and confirm when rooms become available. Include time for checking deliveries and correcting shortages without relying on a generic academic calendar.

Confirm unloading, lift access, carrying routes and who receives each delivery. Recheck allocations after late layout changes. For multiple-property furnishing, keep each site’s labels and delivery instructions distinct even when buying the same range.

Replacement stock and repeat orders

Plan replacement or attic stock by item, not an arbitrary percentage. Review wear, damage history, storage capacity and the disruption caused by an unavailable part. Consider drawer fronts, handles, hinges, glides and matching upholstery as well as complete furniture. Record the location and quantity of each spare and review stock after room turnovers.

Keep supplier codes, finish samples and component references so repeat orders can match the approved range. Ask what happens if a component is discontinued. Discuss minimum order quantities for original and replacement orders without assuming they will be the same. IK ENG can source, inspect, consolidate, warehouse, ship and deliver to site. Frequently asked questions

Is this a complete furniture specification?

No. It is a template. Amend quantities, dimensions, construction, finishes and evidence requirements for each approved room type.

When should we approve a model room?

Approve the final model room and record amendments before bulk manufacture. Use the approved revision for production and inspection.

How much replacement stock should we hold?

Plan by item using wear history, storage capacity and replacement needs. Keep spare stock identified separately from installation stock.
