Outdoor Sauna Planning Permission in the UK

A garden sauna for domestic use can often be treated as an outbuilding under permitted development in England, but this depends on the property, position and design. Check with your council before ordering; different rules apply elsewhere in the UK and to commercial sites.

Garden saunas and permitted development in England

Class E covers buildings incidental to the enjoyment of a dwellinghouse. A domestic garden sauna will often be considered in that category, subject to the full conditions and any restrictions on your property. The following are common screening points, not confirmation that your proposal qualifies:

  • A single-storey outbuilding is generally assessed against an eaves height of 2.5 m and overall height of 4 m for a dual-pitched roof or 3 m for another roof.
  • Within 2 m of a boundary, the commonly cited overall height limit is 2.5 m.
  • The building should not be forward of the wall forming the principal elevation of the original house.
  • Outbuildings and other additions are generally assessed together against a 50% limit on the land around the original house, excluding its footprint. This is not simply half of the lawn visible today.

Confirm the measurements, definitions and remaining conditions against the current GOV.UK permitted development technical guidance. Give your council a site plan, elevations, dimensions and intended use rather than relying on a supplier's description.

Properties and locations where the answer differs

Listed buildings and their grounds need separate advice. Conservation areas, National Parks and Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty (AONBs, also called National Landscapes) have additional restrictions. Planning conditions or an Article 4 direction can remove rights that would otherwise apply.

Flats and maisonettes do not have the same householder permitted development rights. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own rules: check the relevant guidance and your local council rather than applying England's Class E checklist.

Ask about a Lawful Development Certificate

If you want formal certainty about whether your proposed use and building are lawful under planning rules, ask the council about a Lawful Development Certificate. Submit the actual design and position you intend to build. It does not replace building regulations checks or private consents.

Building regulations, electrics and stoves

Small detached outbuildings often do not need building regulations approval, but exemption depends on size, use, construction and location. Ask building control to confirm your proposal. Electrical work still needs to meet the applicable Part P requirements and BS 7671; see our UK sauna electrics guide before choosing a heater.

A wood-burning stove needs a competent installer to assess the flue route, termination, ventilation and clearances from timber and other combustible materials against the stove manual and local requirements. Ask about building control and any smoke-control restrictions before choosing the stove.

Neighbours, ownership and commercial use

Discuss the position, privacy, noise, smoke and access for maintenance with neighbours. Check restrictive covenants, leasehold conditions and any landlord or freeholder consent separately from planning permission.

A holiday-let sauna, hotel installation or other commercial site is more likely to need permission or a separate planning assessment. Do not assume domestic permitted development applies. Explain guest use and operating arrangements to the council before committing.

Prepare your specification before ordering

Agree the base, external dimensions, roof height, heater and delivery access once the site checks are complete. Our sauna sourcing service can work from that specification; planning and installation decisions remain with your council and appointed installers.

Frequently asked questions

Does a garden sauna always need planning permission?

No. A domestic garden sauna may qualify as a permitted development outbuilding in England, but the property, design, position and use all matter. Check your proposal with your local council before ordering.

Are the rules the same across the UK?

No. England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own planning rules. Ask your local council which guidance applies to your site.

Can I get written certainty before buying?

Ask the council about a Lawful Development Certificate for the proposed building and use. Building regulations and private consents still need separate checks.

Does a small sauna exemption cover electrical work?

Do not assume it does. Ask building control about the building and a qualified electrician about Part P, BS 7671 and any notification needed for the electrical installation.
