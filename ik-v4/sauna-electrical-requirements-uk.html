Sauna Electrical Requirements in the UK

Choose a sauna heater with your installer before ordering the cabin. Its power rating, the property supply and the cable route determine what installation is practical, and a qualified electrician should assess all three against the heater manual.

Match the heater to the cabin volume

Heater output in kW is sized to cabin volume using the factory specification and heater manual. Give the supplier the internal dimensions, glazing and construction details so the proposed heater can be checked against the manufacturer's sizing instructions. Do not select it from the number of seats alone.

Check the property supply before ordering

Many electric heaters need a dedicated circuit. Some models need three-phase power or a larger supply than the property currently has. Your electrician should review the proposed heater and controller, existing supply capacity and other loads before confirming what can be connected.

Ask for the electrical requirements early enough to resolve any supply changes with the relevant network operator. A heater's availability does not mean it can run from your existing socket or consumer unit.

Why a sauna needs specific electrical design

BS 7671 has a dedicated section for locations containing sauna heaters. In plain terms, the electrician must consider temperature zones, suitable cable types and the applicable RCD protection requirements. Heat affects where equipment and cables can go, so ordinary room wiring assumptions are not enough.

Ask the electrician to use the current edition and applicable amendments, together with the manufacturer's instructions. The IET's BS 7671 information is a starting point for the standard; installation design belongs with the qualified electrician.

Outdoor cable routes and Part P notification

An outdoor sauna needs a suitable supply run designed and installed by a qualified electrician. This may involve armoured cable and a trench, depending on the route and site. Agree the route and any groundwork before preparing the base; let the electrician specify the cable and protection rather than digging to a generic depth from an online guide.

Part P applies to domestic electrical work in England and Wales, with different notification rules. A new circuit is normally notifiable; ask your electrician to confirm the applicable process and arrange notification through their competent person scheme or building control as appropriate. See England's Approved Document P and check the Welsh requirements where relevant. Scotland and Northern Ireland have separate arrangements.

Infrared panels and combination units

Infrared cabins often have a lower total electrical load than traditional heater cabins, but the actual model rating governs. Fixed wiring must follow the manual and the electrician's design; do not assume every infrared cabin is suitable for a standard socket. Combination infrared and traditional units need assessment of their permitted operating modes and total load.

Documentation for imported heaters and controls

Before ordering, request UKCA or CE marking information, relevant conformity documentation, English manuals, wiring diagrams and control panel documentation for the exact heater and controller. Give the pack to your electrician to review, including sensor positions and installation instructions.

IK ENG checks that the documentation is supplied. It does not certify products. Our sauna sourcing page explains how to send the cabin and heater requirements with your brief.

Wood-burning stoves still need installation planning

A wood-burning sauna stove itself needs no electrical supply, although lighting or other accessories may. A competent installer must assess the flue and clearances against the stove instructions and local requirements. For outdoor cabins, also read the planning permission guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can a sauna heater use an ordinary socket?

Do not assume so. Many heaters need a dedicated circuit, and some need three-phase power or a larger supply. A qualified electrician must check the exact model and the property supply.

How is the heater power selected?

Use the factory and heater manual sizing instructions for the cabin volume and construction, including glazing. Confirm the proposed heater with the supplier and installer before ordering.

Does IK ENG certify the heater?

No. IK ENG checks that UKCA or CE marking information, relevant documentation, English manuals, wiring diagrams and control panel documentation are supplied. It does not certify products.

Does an infrared cabin avoid electrical installation checks?

No. Infrared cabins often have a lower load, but the model rating and manual still govern the supply and any fixed wiring. Ask a qualified electrician to confirm the installation.
