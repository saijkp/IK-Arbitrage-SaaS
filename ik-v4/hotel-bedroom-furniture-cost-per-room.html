  • Import VAT at 20%, plus duty where it applies: see our furniture import duty and VAT guide.

The VAT allowance uses the GOV.UK standard VAT rate. Ask the broker to confirm the commodity classification and applicable treatment through the UK Trade Tariff service. Import VAT valuation includes relevant import charges and incidental expenses; use HMRC's import VAT valuation guidance rather than applying VAT only to the furniture line.

The shipping estimate belongs to the stated order size. A different mix of furniture, packing or delivery requirements needs a fresh logistics estimate. Keep the furniture, fee, freight and tax lines visible when comparing proposals, so an omitted category cannot look like a saving.

These are not contract-grade hotel prices

The figures above are not contract-grade hotel prices. Upholstered headboards, fire-compliant seating, luggage racks and similar items are priced from the hotel’s own room schedule. Do not treat a photograph, a domestic product description or the table as confirmation that a piece is suitable for guest use.

A practical hotel schedule starts with how the room will operate: guest turnover, cleaning methods, luggage handling and likely wear. State the intended use before asking for options. Our FF&E sourcing service can coordinate the broader schedule, keeping bedroom casegoods, upholstery and other categories identifiable.

What drives the furniture price range?

  • Board and material: record the board type, thickness, exposed edges, back panels and any solid timber or veneer requirements. A finish name alone does not describe the construction.
  • Finish: specify colour, texture, sheen and edge treatment. Compare a physical finish sample with the room palette and your cleaning products before approving it.
  • Drawer runners and hinges: name the hardware or agree its required performance. Check drawer extension, closing action, adjustment and access for replacement.
  • Sizes and internal layout: confirm overall dimensions, usable storage and clearances. A wider wardrobe or altered desk arrangement changes the specification being priced.
  • Quantity: show quantities by item and room type. Repeated pieces and numerous variations create different production and packing requirements.

Ask suppliers to price the same written specification. If an alternative reduces the price, identify exactly what changed: substrate, edging, hardware, dimensions or finish. Keep accepted alternatives on the schedule so the sample, production order and inspection brief describe the same furniture.

Contract use: durability, spares and upholstery

Check vulnerable details before choosing a range. Bed joints should be accessible for maintenance; drawer bases, handles and desk edges need scrutiny. Ask how damaged components can be replaced without replacing an entire unit. Confirm the cleaning method and request evidence for any durability claim you intend to rely on.

Discuss replacement stock alongside the main order. Identify which handles, hinges, runners, panels and finishes you want recorded for future orders, and ask whether matching replacements can be supplied. Do not assume a later production batch will match an approved colour automatically. Retain the final specification and a labelled finish sample.

For upholstered items, agree the fire-performance requirement before selection. The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations address domestic upholstered furniture; they are not a universal hotel contract specification. Current government guidance on the furniture regulations explains their domestic scope and amendments.

For hotels in England, consult the Home Office sleeping-accommodation fire-risk guidance. Agree the appropriate specification with your fire-risk assessor and obtain evidence covering the actual upholstery construction supplied. Confirm the guidance applicable to your UK nation and premises. HMO operators can also use our HMO furniture fire-safety checklist; a hotel and an HMO should not automatically share an upholstery specification.

How to get an accurate price

  1. Build a room schedule. List each room type, every item, quantities, dimensions and whether you are retaining existing furniture. Include accessible rooms and unusual layouts rather than assuming every room is identical.
  2. Send drawings. Show doors, windows, sockets, skirting and clearances. Confirm the bed frame's intended mattress fit and check wardrobe doors and desk chairs against the available space.
  3. Approve samples. Agree board, finish, edging, handles and upholstery evidence. Our guide to ordering samples explains how to turn samples into an approval record.
  4. Review a model room. Check the proposed pieces together, including storage access, guest circulation, housekeeping access and the appearance of adjoining finishes. Record changes before repeating the layout.
  5. Confirm the order scope. Request an itemised proposal identifying the furniture specification, fee, logistics, taxes and exclusions, together with approval and delivery milestones.

For mixed residential layouts, our serviced-apartment sourcing service and whole-house furniture package approach help organise rooms beyond the bedroom. Use the package builder to assemble an initial brief, then attach your schedule and drawings for the details a product selection cannot capture.

Shipping and the delivery programme

For sea freight, allow roughly 5 weeks port to port. That describes the sea leg only. Agree production, approval, collection, customs clearance and site delivery milestones separately before setting a refurbishment programme.

Include access constraints in the brief: delivery postcode, vehicle access, unloading arrangements, stairs or lifts and where cartons can be received. Agree who checks the delivered items against the room schedule and who assembles them. The furniture-only figures above do not include assembly.
