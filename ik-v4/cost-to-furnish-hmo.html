How Much Does It Cost to Furnish an HMO?

Start with the rooms and the specification, then build the delivered budget. A price per bedroom only helps if you know what it includes. This guide uses IK ENG's published planning estimates and shows how to turn them into a practical HMO purchasing brief.

Start with a room schedule

Walk through the property room by room. Record the bedroom count, bed sizes, storage needs and whether each room needs a desk. List shared living and dining furniture separately. Kitchens, fitted wardrobes, lighting and flooring need their own measured schedules. This prevents the bedroom allowance from quietly absorbing items that serve the whole property.

Measure the usable space rather than simply copying the floor area from an estate agent's plan. Allow room for doors and drawers to open and check the delivery route. A wardrobe that fits the wall may still be difficult to take around a tight staircase. Give each room a reference so drawings, quotations and carton labels use the same names.

The figures below come from our budget planning guide. They are rough planning estimates, not fixed HMO prices. They exclude the sourcing fee, freight, duty, UK delivery and installation. Confirm the VAT treatment separately when comparing quotations.

Not every HMO bedroom needs two bedside units or a freestanding wardrobe. Use these packages as a starting point, then remove or replace items on the schedule. A smaller room might need compact storage; another may justify a larger desk. Quote the actual mix rather than multiplying a generic pack without checking the layouts.

A worked six-bedroom example

This is an illustration, not a completed IK ENG project or a customer quotation. Six standard bedroom packages at £1,000–£2,500 each give a goods-only allowance of £6,000–£15,000. Add one standard living room at £2,000–£5,000 and one dining area at £1,000–£3,000. That produces £9,000–£23,000 for those furniture groups.

At our 5% sourcing fee, the fee on that illustrative product value would be £450–£1,150. The combined goods-and-fee subtotal would therefore be £9,450–£24,150 before freight, duty, VAT where applicable, delivery and installation. It also excludes kitchen cabinetry, bathroom products, curtains, appliances and any desk not already listed. Keep those omissions visible rather than presenting the subtotal as a furnished-property price.

Build the delivered budget

Ask for separate lines for products, the sourcing fee, samples, inspection where applicable, freight, insurance, customs clearance, duty and final delivery. Check how import VAT and any VAT on UK services are shown. A factory price and a delivered quotation describe different scopes, so neither should be compared without the missing costs.

The physical volume of furniture matters as well as its value. Packed dimensions, the mix of assembled and flat-pack products, the number of suppliers and the delivery address all affect logistics. Confirm these details before treating an early estimate as the amount you need in the bank. Our furniture import duty and VAT guide explains the tax questions to resolve.

Choose where to spend

Prioritise the items tenants use daily. Set the bed and mattress specification, usable storage and practical dining provision first. Record materials, hardware, dimensions and finishes so competing quotations describe comparable products. A lower number with a different mattress, thinner cabinet panels or fewer pieces may simply be a different package.

Decorative extras can be a separate optional list. This lets you approve the essentials before deciding on mirrors, occasional tables or additional accessories. Ask about replacement handles and other parts during sourcing. Keeping product references and finish samples makes later purchasing easier, even when you choose a different supplier for an urgent replacement.

Keep compliance in the buying schedule

Identify upholstered products and mattress requirements when selecting items, rather than waiting for shipment. IK ENG provides BS 7177 evidence for mattresses and beds and permanent fire labels on relevant upholstered items, with test certificates in the compliance pack. Match documents to the approved product references and retain them with the property inventory.

Read our HMO furniture fire-safety guide for the difference between upholstery labels and mattress documentation. A furniture budget is more useful when it covers the products you can actually approve and keep records for, rather than an attractive photograph with an incomplete specification.

Plan timing and payment

Standard items typically take 10–12 weeks to site; bespoke items take 12–18 weeks from approved drawings. Leave time before that for decisions, measurements and sample approval. Allow time after delivery for assembly, checks and cleaning before tenants move in. The shipping and production guide explains the stages.

Our payment terms are a 50% deposit to place the order and the balance on delivery. Plan the cash flow against the itemised quotation, including when separately listed logistics costs are due. There is no minimum to enquire. Factory minimum quantities and shipping practicality are checked against the actual products.
