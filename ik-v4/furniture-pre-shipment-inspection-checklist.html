Furniture inspection checklist for orders from China

Use this checklist for furniture, kitchen cabinets and wardrobes before they leave the factory. Agree the reference documents, access and decision process before booking an inspection.

Set the reference before production starts

A useful furniture inspection checklist for China starts with an agreed product definition. Give the inspector the purchase order, item codes, quantities, approved sample, signed drawings and specification sheet. Record revision numbers and dates. A photograph in a catalogue cannot settle a disagreement about board thickness, hinge type or an internal shelf position.

Keep a reference sample or signed finish panel available at the factory. Identify which document takes priority if the sample and drawing differ, and resolve the difference before inspection. Our guide to ordering samples from China explains how to record approvals. Add agreed tolerances and viewing conditions rather than letting the inspector invent them on the day.

When to inspect: production and pre-shipment

A during-production visit is useful while construction, materials and unfinished surfaces remain visible. It gives the factory a chance to correct repeated mistakes before an entire order is completed. It does not replace a final check of finished goods and packing. For fitted products, review the drawings before manufacture as well: inspection cannot fix a wardrobe designed for the wrong room dimensions.

For a pre-shipment inspection, agree the readiness threshold in writing. One possible booking condition is 100% of goods produced and at least 80% packed; this is an example to agree, not an IK ENG guarantee or universal rule. Record the actual percentages on arrival. If only part of the order is available, identify what was excluded and decide whether to postpone or inspect again.

Allow access to sealed cartons, space for assembly and staff to repack correctly. Arrange the visit before shipment release, leaving time for rework and re-inspection. Tell the supplier who can authorise release; the inspector records evidence and should not be assumed to approve commercial compromises.

Quantity, identity and dimensions

  • Reconcile ordered units, completed units and packed cartons against the packing list. A cabinet with three cartons is one unit, not three cabinets.
  • Check item codes, colours, left/right handing and room references. Identify missing fittings and cartons belonging to another order.
  • Measure overall height, width and depth against the approved drawing, then important openings, shelf positions and connection points.
  • Record the measured value, drawing value and agreed tolerance. Photograph the measuring tool in position so the reading has context.

For kitchens, check appliance openings, door alignment, service cut-outs and filler pieces. Use the kitchen cabinet checklist to prepare the specification. For wardrobes, verify carcasses, door travel, internal layout and installation clearances against the approved measuring and drawing pack. Factory measurements do not replace a reliable site survey.

Materials, construction and finish

Compare accessible components with the specified material and thickness. Look at edges, undersides, backs and internal faces as well as the front. Record gaps, chips, cracks, loose joints, exposed fixings and damaged edge banding. A visual check cannot reliably prove the composition of concealed materials; request supporting documents or specialist testing where the specification needs that evidence.

Compare colour, sheen and texture with the approved sample under consistent lighting. Check adjacent doors and panels together, because a difference may be obvious across a kitchen run even when individual pieces look acceptable. Photograph both the wider surface and the detail. Do not use a close-up alone to judge whether a mark is visible in normal use.

Hardware, assembly and function

Verify the agreed hardware make or model where specified, along with fixing quantities and instructions. Check hinges, drawer runners, handles, shelf supports, hanging rails and adjustable feet. Open and close doors and drawers through their travel; look for binding, rubbing, loose fixings and uneven gaps. Confirm soft-close functions only where ordered.

Assemble selected products using the supplied parts and instructions. Record which item codes were assembled and which were only visually checked. Check alignment, stability and access to fixings without improvising unsafe load tests. Where a formal strength or durability test is required, agree the test method and suitable facility separately. Repack the assembled sample and confirm the fittings remain with the correct unit.

Upholstered furniture: labels and supporting evidence

For domestic upholstered furniture within scope, the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988, as amended, set flammability requirements. Check the applicable permanent label and record its wording, attachment and product identity. The https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/furniture-and-furnishings-fire-safety-regulations/furniture-and-furnishings-fire-safety-amendment-regulations-2025-uk.UK guidance on the 2025 amendment confirms that display or swing-label requirements were removed, while permanent-label obligations remain.

Ask for test evidence that matches the supplied product and upholstery specification. Label photographs alone cannot demonstrate fire performance, and an inspection visit is not a laboratory fire test. Refer scope, exceptions and suitability questions to a competent specialist before release. Our HMO furniture fire-safety guide gives further context for landlords.

Carton marks, packing and drop risk

Check carton identity, carton sequence, handling marks and the link to the packing list. Open agreed samples to examine corner protection, separated hardware, protected glass and surfaces that might rub together. Look for voids that allow movement, crushed edges, moisture or straps pressing into finished panels. Record carton dimensions and gross weights for shipping planning.

Discuss the handling route and whether the packaging can tolerate stacking and repeated transfers. Any drop test needs an agreed method, supplier permission and a safe area; do not improvise one with fragile or heavy furniture. Record packing changes, then check that replacement packing still protects the goods and that the packing list reflects any changed carton sizes.

Sampling and an example report layout

AQL means acceptance quality limit. ISO 2859-1 describes sampling inspection by attributes. A sampling plan needs an agreed lot definition, inspection level and acceptance criteria before use. Sampling does not check every item or guarantee a defect-free shipment. Do not treat a sample result as a measured defect rate for the entire order.

Photographs, defects and shipment release

Use a consistent photo sequence: carton mark, complete item, defect location, close-up and measurement where relevant. Name files so they link to the report row and item code. Keep original images alongside the report, approved references and supplier replies. State inaccessible areas and checks not performed instead of leaving the reader to assume they passed.

For each defect, agree the correction, responsible person and evidence required. Hold affected goods from shipment while unresolved issues are reviewed. Rework may require checking other goods made in the same way, not just the photographed item. After correction, re-inspect the relevant checks and confirm that repacking has not created another problem. Obtain written release from the authorised buyer before loading.

Frequently asked questions

When should furniture be inspected before shipment?

Agree a readiness threshold before booking. Record how much is produced and packed, and allow time for rework and re-inspection before release.

Does inspection prove upholstered furniture is fire-safe?

No. Visual checks and label photographs do not replace relevant test evidence or specialist review of the supplied upholstery specification.

What happens if defects are found?

Record the evidence, agree corrective action and hold affected goods from shipment until the authorised buyer reviews re-inspection evidence and releases them.
