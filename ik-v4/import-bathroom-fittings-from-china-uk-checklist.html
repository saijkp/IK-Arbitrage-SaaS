Import bathroom fittings from China: UK checklist

Import bathroom fittings from China with a UK checklist for water pressure, connections, WCs, vanity units, electrical safety, samples and spare parts.

Plan bathroom sourcing

Start with the room and installer

To import bathroom fittings from China UK buyers need a compatibility brief before a factory quotation. A photograph cannot tell you whether a mixer will work on the supply, whether a WC fits the soil outlet or whether a mirror suits its proposed location. Treat the bathroom as a connected assembly, with one approved schedule for plumbing, furniture and electrical items.

Give your plumber and electrician the measured plan, existing services and proposed products. Record finished wall and floor levels, pipe positions, door swings, access panels and maintenance clearances. For repeat bathrooms, identify every room type and exception. Ask the factory to quote against drawings and product codes rather than descriptions such as “standard UK bathroom”.

Water fittings requirements and evidence

The Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999 apply in England and Wales and address the suitability and installation of water fittings. Confirm the applicable requirements for the project location elsewhere in the UK. Ask your installer to review the proposed fittings, backflow protection and installation arrangement before you approve manufacture.

WRAS approval is a common way to show product compliance, rather than the only route. WRAS explains its product approvals and notes that other approval schemes are available. Request evidence for the exact model, not just a factory logo. Check its current listing and installation conditions. An approved component does not approve an entire bathroom installation; retain the evidence with your purchase specification.

Pressure, flow and connections

Check the product's minimum operating pressure. A low-pressure gravity system and a combi or unvented system can need different product selections. Ask the plumber to record available pressure and flow under realistic use, then compare those figures with the manufacturer's operating range. A large shower head can disappoint if the supply cannot deliver its required flow.

For mixers and showers, confirm hot and cold supply compatibility, controls, hose connections and access to service concealed valves. Agree which components come in the box. Obtain dimensioned drawings of every inlet, outlet, thread and sealing arrangement. Have the installer confirm thread type and size against the UK pipework; do not assume two nominal sizes use the same connection.

WC outlets, cisterns and waste routes

Measure the soil outlet from finished surfaces and draw the intended pan connector route. Check whether the pan outlet is horizontal or vertical, available clearance and the connection to the cistern. A close-coupled pan, concealed cistern and wall-hung frame are not interchangeable. Confirm seat fixing centres, replacement seat availability, flush controls and access to inlet and outlet valves.

For basins, record waste size, overflow design, trap arrangement and the route through the vanity. Confirm whether the supplied waste suits a basin with or without an overflow. Ask the installer to approve the trap and downstream pipe connection sizes. Drawers must clear the trap without improvised cuts on site. Include seals, brackets and fixing details in the approval pack.

Vanity moisture resistance and maintenance

Specify the carcass material, surface finish, edge sealing and treatment of holes and cut-outs. “Waterproof” in a product title is not a usable material specification. Ask how the unit should be cleaned and how water around the basin joint is managed. Inspect the underside and back as well as the visible door finish: exposed edges are easy to miss in catalogue images.

Choose a sample finish and check it against taps, handles, basin colour and lighting. Confirm the wall construction and fixing method with the installer, especially for wall-hung units. Keep shut-off valves and waste connections accessible. Agree how a damaged door, drawer front or handle can be replaced without changing the complete unit.

Electrical items: UKCA and bathroom zones

For this purchasing checklist, require UKCA marking for electrical mirrors, electric towel rails and fans, with the relevant declaration and supporting product documents. Use GOV.UK electrical equipment safety guidance to check the UKCA route and importer obligations. A marking alone does not establish suitability for the proposed bathroom position. Define the destination market before ordering; this checklist focuses on Great Britain.

Have a qualified electrician select the bathroom zone IP ratings for each exact position, review power supplies and controls, and install the equipment. Approved Document P covers electrical safety in dwellings in England and references installation standards for special locations. Ask the electrician to record the required IP rating on the schedule rather than accepting a general “bathroom suitable” claim. Confirm local requirements for projects elsewhere.

For a lit mirror, check driver replacement access, demister controls and cable entry. For a fan, agree the duct route and control method before choosing a model. For a towel rail, clarify heating type and electrical connection. Our UKCA lighting guide helps organise the document request.

Spare parts, samples and inspection

Request cartridge model numbers, valve drawings, aerators, seals, hoses, shower controls and WC flush parts. Agree which spares you will hold and how to reorder them. A cartridge described only by diameter may still have different locating features. Ask for photographs and dimensions, and keep a labelled spare with its matching product code.

Follow our sample ordering guide and approve an assembled combination where fit matters: tap with basin, waste with trap, or pan with cistern. Have the installer review it before the bulk order. Record finish, dimensions, included accessories and acceptable workmanship using photographs and a signed revision. Keep a reference sample for comparison.

Use sourcing inspection and visits to plan checks against the approved specification. Include counts, damage, critical dimensions, accessory packs, labels and packing protection. Agree functional checks in advance. Inspection supports quality control; it does not replace compliance evidence or installation checks.

From approval to site delivery

Freeze product codes and drawings before production. Label cartons by bathroom type and item, with a packing list distinguishing fragile ceramics from furniture and electrical products. Agree delivery access, unloading responsibility and a place to check goods before the installation team needs them. Record damage and missing accessories against carton numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Can I choose taps from photographs alone?

No. Check exact model drawings, minimum operating pressure, connections and compliance evidence with your installer before ordering.

Does WRAS approval cover the whole installation?

No. Check the exact approved product and its installation conditions. The complete arrangement still needs review by your installer.

Should I order spare cartridges?

Agree spare cartridges and other service parts before purchase. Keep model references, dimensions and a labelled sample so replacements match.
