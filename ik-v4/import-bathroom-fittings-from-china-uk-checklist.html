Import bathroom fittings from China: UK checklist

Check compatibility before ordering taps, showers, sanitaryware and bathroom furniture. Use this checklist with your plumber and electrician to turn a product list into an approved specification.

Plan your bathroom sourcing

Start with the installed bathroom, not the catalogue

To import bathroom fittings from China UK buyers need a coordinated specification: product drawings, site measurements, water-system details and the installer's approval. A tap that looks right can still perform poorly on the available pressure; a WC can fit the room but miss the soil connection. Resolve those interfaces before a deposit commits you to a production run.

Give each bathroom a room reference and each product a model code. Record quantities, finish, dimensions, mounting method and all included accessories. Mark what the factory supplies and what the UK installer supplies. For repeated bathrooms, keep a separate list of exceptions such as handed vanity units, different wall build-ups or concealed cistern access.

Water fittings requirements and evidence

The Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999, regulation 4 require appropriate quality, suitability for the circumstances and compliance with the applicable installation requirements. These regulations apply in England and Wales; confirm the applicable local water requirements for projects elsewhere in the UK. Ask your plumber to review the exact products and proposed installation, including backflow protection, before ordering.

https://www.wrasapprovals.co.uk/approvals/products_and_materials_directory/?cms=true approval is a common route used to demonstrate product compliance. The https://www.wrasapprovals.co.uk/approvals/products_and_materials_directory/?cms=true approvals directory describes approved fittings as complying when correctly installed. Check the exact model, approval scope, validity and installation conditions rather than accepting a logo on a supplier's brochure. Approval for one cartridge or material should not be treated as approval for every assembled tap in the range. Installation suitability remains a separate check.

Taps, mixers and showers: check pressure and connections

Tell the supplier whether the property has a low-pressure gravity system, a combi boiler or an unvented system. Check the product's minimum operating pressure and ask the plumber to confirm available pressure and flow at the outlet. Do not assume that a tall mixer, rainfall head or thermostatic shower will work well just because its inlet can be connected.

Request performance information for the complete shower arrangement, including the intended head, hose and diverter. Check hot and cold supply requirements, service access and whether simultaneous outlets change performance. Keep the manufacturer's operating limits with the schedule so a later model substitution cannot quietly change the requirement.

Confirm thread standard, nominal connection sizes, male or female ends, hose lengths and sealing method on drawings. Ask the installer to approve any adapters and isolation arrangements. Check tap-hole diameter, deck thickness, fixing reach and spout projection over the basin. A photograph does not establish connection compatibility.

WCs, basins and wastes: approve the whole assembly

For every WC, compare the outlet direction, outlet position and pan dimensions with the site soil connection and proposed pan connector. Confirm the required clearance behind and beside the pan. With a wall-hung pan, check frame fixing centres, load support, outlet alignment and the finished wall thickness against the selected frame.

Check cistern and pan compatibility as a pair: flush connection, fixing points, flush mechanism and accessible servicing. For concealed systems, approve the flush plate and access opening together. Record the seat fixing arrangement and replacement seat reference. Ask for drawings of the actual supplied assembly rather than a similar showroom model.

For basins and shower trays, confirm waste opening size, waste fitting, trap connection, overflow arrangement and space for the trap. Record the waste sizes explicitly and have the plumber match them to the site pipework. Establish whether each basin needs a slotted or unslotted waste; check the chosen waste against the basin's overflow design. Avoid specifying a universal waste without drawings.

Vanity units and mirrors: moisture and maintenance

Specify the vanity carcass and door materials, edge treatment, surface finish and sealing around cut-outs. Ask how exposed edges, sink joints and fixing holes are protected against moisture. A water-resistant surface does not establish that the entire unit will tolerate standing water. Request cleaning instructions and inspect vulnerable joints on the sample.

Check drawer clearance around plumbing, access to isolation valves and wall-fixing requirements. Confirm mirror dimensions, mounting points and clearance for doors or cabinets. Agree the finish sample under the intended room lighting; keep a labelled reference for later replacements. Check that handles, runners and hinges can be replaced without dismantling the bathroom.

Electrical mirrors, heated towel rails and extractor fans

Set UKCA marking and supporting conformity documentation as your purchasing requirement for electrical items. GOV.UK electrical equipment safety guidance explains the UKCA route and importer obligations. Obtain model-specific declarations, instructions and relevant test evidence for review; a mark alone does not establish suitability for a bathroom.

Have a qualified electrician specify the bathroom zone and required IP rating for each proposed location, then confirm the exact product is suitable before purchase. Arrange installation by a qualified electrician. Approved Document P provides official electrical safety guidance for dwellings in England; have the installer confirm the applicable rules for your project location. Do not select an IP rating from a room photograph.

For illuminated mirrors, check supply, driver location, demister controls and service access. For towel rails, distinguish wet heating, electric and dual-fuel models, confirming valves or heating elements and controls as applicable. For fans, agree duct connection, airflow requirement, controls, noise expectations and maintenance access with the designer. Include mounting kits and instructions in the approval pack.

Samples, spare parts and inspection

Order a working sample of critical taps, shower controls and wastes, and trial the chosen combinations with your installer. Use our sample-ordering guide to document what is being approved. Ask for cartridge dimensions and reference codes, aerators, seals, hoses, flush valves, seats and matching finishes. Agree how future parts will be identified and supplied, without assuming continued stock availability.

Build the inspection brief from the signed schedule. Check model and finish labels, dimensions, visible defects, included fittings, carton protection and quantities. Agree any functional checks in advance and retain photographs linked to the product codes. Inspection cannot replace product compliance evidence or the installer's assessment. See sourcing inspection and visits for support.

Release the order and plan site delivery

Freeze drawings, finishes and the bill of included accessories before bulk production. Require written approval for substitutions. Pack ceramics and mirrors with agreed protection and identify cartons by room and item. Prepare a receiving checklist so missing traps or fixing kits are noticed while the delivery is being checked, rather than during installation.

Keep electrical documentation with the relevant product records; our UKCA lighting guide provides further document checks. For the furniture portion of a mixed order, use the furniture import duty guide as a separate planning reference. Do not assume its classifications apply to sanitaryware or electrical products.

Frequently asked questions

What should I check before importing bathroom fittings?

Confirm the exact model, water-system suitability, minimum operating pressure, connections, dimensions and installation requirements with your installer before ordering.

Does WRAS approval cover every product in a range?

Check the exact model and approval scope in the WRAS directory. Do not assume approval for one component or model covers the entire range or installation.

Can I choose bathroom electrical items by appearance alone?

Have a qualified electrician confirm the bathroom zone, required IP rating and installation suitability, and review UKCA documentation before purchase.
