How Long Does Shipping from China to the UK Take?

The useful question is when your approved order can reach the property, rather than how long a ship is at sea. Build a programme that includes production, inspection, consolidation, freight, customs and the final delivery appointment.

Use a door-to-site planning window

IK ENG's standard items typically take 10–12 weeks to site. Bespoke items typically take 12–18 weeks from approved drawings. These are overall order-planning windows, not sailing times. Treat the required arrival date as the end of a chain of decisions and physical movements.

Before ordering, separate the date you send an enquiry from the date the specification is approved. Time spent changing sizes, choosing finishes or waiting for measurements belongs in your project programme too. If a room must be ready for a tenancy or a handover, include assembly, checking and cleaning after the delivery date.

Stage one: brief, options and approvals

Send a room list, floor plans, reference images, postcode, approximate budget and required date. IK ENG returns factory options and pricing within 48 hours of a brief. That gives you a starting point for selection; it is not the point at which every product has already been manufactured.

Use the next step to settle the schedule: dimensions, quantities, materials, finish references and hardware. For kitchens or fitted wardrobes, review measured drawings and appliance details. Nominate one person to approve revisions so factories do not receive conflicting instructions from different people on the project.

Stage two: production

Standard and bespoke products can follow different manufacturing programmes. Factory capacity, the approved specification and the mix of products determine the order sequence. For a whole-house package, identify which items take longest and which can be prepared while another drawing is being finalised.

Keep the approved revision visible on the order. A late change to a door finish or cabinet width may affect more than one component. Ask for the impact on the programme before approving the change, and update the room schedule so production and inspection teams work from the same information.

Stage three: inspection and corrections

Every IK ENG order is inspected before shipping against the approved specification, with a photo report. Agree the checks in advance: product references, quantities, dimensions, finish and packing details should reflect the order. Give the inspector the approved version rather than a collection of old messages.

Leave room in the programme to resolve anything found during inspection. A report is most useful while the goods remain accessible for correction. Confirm who reviews it and how quickly they can make a decision. A delivery deadline should not depend on everyone being available at short notice without a named owner.

Stage four: consolidation and packing

A coordinated interiors order may involve several factories. Consolidation brings those goods together for the agreed shipment. Check which suppliers must finish before the shipment can leave and whether a split order is practical if one bespoke item is still in production.

Label cartons by property, unit and room where useful. Keep packing lists consistent with the product schedule and record items needing special handling. Fragile stone, mirrors and large assembled furniture need a packing and receiving plan. Agree those details while the goods can still be packed to match the delivery route.

Stage five: sea freight

The sea leg is only part of the lead time. Booking availability, routing and port operations can affect the programme. Track the actual booking milestones rather than assuming that a general estimate is the confirmed departure or arrival date for your shipment.

Ask for updates at meaningful points: cargo ready, booking confirmed, departure, expected arrival and any revised routing. Keep the site team informed when those milestones change. If you are planning furniture packs for property developers, relate updates to the relevant block or handover phase.

Stage six: customs and UK delivery

Prepare the commercial invoice, packing list and product details before arrival. Confirm the importer and the arrangements for duty and VAT with the customs agent. Missing information is easier to resolve before the shipment reaches the point where someone needs it urgently. Our duty and VAT guide covers the budget and paperwork questions.

Book the final delivery around actual access. Confirm the postcode, parking, unloading space, receiving contact and any restrictions on vehicle size or delivery hours. An empty property still needs someone to receive goods and somewhere dry and secure to put them. Check which handling and assembly services are included.

Work backwards from the room-ready date

Start with the date rooms must be ready. Set aside the time your team needs for assembly, snagging, cleaning and photography where relevant. Put the delivery milestone before that, then work backwards through the order window and the approvals that must happen before production.

For bespoke items, use the approved-drawings milestone explicitly. Write down who measures, who signs off and when. This is particularly useful for kitchen cabinet sourcing and fitted wardrobes and joinery, where an unresolved dimension can hold up the order.

Air freight for genuinely urgent items

Air freight is available for urgent items. It can help with a suitable small product or critical component, but it does not remove manufacturing, packing, paperwork or final delivery. Check the actual item, dimensions and required date before treating it as a recovery plan.

Keep urgent items separate in the brief. A replacement part and a full room package are different logistics questions. For an immediate tenancy change, local UK stock may be more practical than a new China order. Decide this early so one urgent item does not distort the plan for the rest of the property.

Make the order programme easy to follow

Use a short tracker with each product group, its approval date, production status, inspection milestone and planned shipment. Name the person responsible for the next decision. Review changes against the site date, rather than collecting updates without checking what they mean for handover.
