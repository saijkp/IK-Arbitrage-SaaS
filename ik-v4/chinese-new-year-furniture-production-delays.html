Chinese New Year furniture production delays

Chinese New Year furniture production delays can affect the whole route from approved sample to UK site delivery. For landlords, developers, hotels and designers, the useful deadline is when complete, checked goods must arrive for installation. Work backwards from that date and obtain a written holiday programme from every supplier.

Plan your furniture sourcing

Annual date check: Chinese New Year 2027 factory closure

Chinese New Year falls on Saturday 6 February 2027, confirmed by the Hong Kong government's general holidays list. This verifies New Year's Day; it does not establish mainland China's public holiday length or any factory's closure dates.

For mainland public holiday arrangements, check the official PRC State Council notice. For your order, ask each supplier for its own closure and restart dates in writing. Recheck this section when planning a later year's purchase.

Why the production gap extends beyond the holiday

Widely reported manufacturing patterns include staff travelling home before the holiday, output slowing before closure and a gradual restart afterwards. Some staff may not return to the same employer. Factories often stop for longer than the official public holiday, but there is no universal closure period. DHL's factory restart guidance describes staggered staff returns and supplier readiness as separate constraints.

Treat an office reopening, a production line restarting and your goods becoming ready for collection as different milestones. Ask who will confirm each one. An email saying “back after the holiday” is not enough to schedule fitters, release a hotel room or promise a tenant a furnished property.

Check the suppliers behind your supplier

Your assembly factory may depend on other businesses for boards, upholstery fabric, hinges, drawer runners, glass, bathroom components or lighting parts. Raw material and component suppliers also close. Ask which materials are already available for your order and which still depend on another supplier restarting. A finished carcass without its agreed hardware is not a completed kitchen.

Record the approved specification and require written approval before any substitution. For furniture, identify the finish and fabric reference; for kitchens, confirm drawings and hardware; for bathrooms, confirm the exact fitting and finish; for lighting, confirm the model and agreed product documents. Decide who can approve changes while your usual contact is away.

Allow for backlogs, freight space and collection cut-offs

Pre-holiday factory backlogs can put competing orders ahead of yours. Ask whether your production slot is confirmed and what could move it. DHL's holiday planning guidance reports pressure on freight space and port congestion around the period. These are planning risks, not a prediction of a particular rate or delay for your shipment.

Confirm the last collection date as well as the factory's closing date. Goods must be packed, checked and available while the people arranging handover are still working. The forwarder should confirm booking status, cargo acceptance and document deadlines. A supplier's promise to finish before closure does not by itself secure a sailing.

A planning template based on your own site date

Sea freight is roughly 5 weeks port to port. This covers the sea leg only, so agree separate allowances for the other stages.

Start with your required on-site date and the installation sequence. Build the programme below using supplier and forwarder commitments. Write the agreed calendar date beside each stage; leave no stage as an assumed standard lead time. Use our China to UK shipping times guide to distinguish production from transport.

  1. Required on-site date: confirm when each room or trade needs its goods, access arrangements and who accepts delivery. Work back from installation and snagging to the delivery milestone.
  2. Before that delivery: agree the shipping plan and allowances for collection, handling, clearance and the final site appointment with the forwarder. Sea freight is roughly 5 weeks port to port; that is not the complete order-to-site programme.
  3. Before the agreed cargo cut-off: schedule pre-shipment inspection, correction of defects, any reinspection, packing and collection. Aim to ship before the factory closes where the confirmed programme allows it.
  4. Before inspection: obtain a production completion date tied to the approved specification. Confirm material availability and the factory's holiday capacity before relying on the slot.
  5. Before production starts: confirm specifications, drawings and samples, then place the deposit under the agreed order terms. Ask the supplier what actually reserves capacity and record its acknowledgement.

If any agreed date falls after the relevant supplier or logistics cut-off, revise the programme before placing the order. Do not fill the gap with an optimistic estimate. Discuss an earlier approval, a phased delivery or a different product with confirmed availability.

Approve samples and book inspection before the break

Arrange sample delivery, review and approval while the factory can still answer questions and make corrections. Keep an approved reference and send a clear approval record to the supplier. Our guide to ordering samples from China explains how to make the sample part of the production specification.

Book inspections before the break and agree a fallback if production slips. Confirm what must be complete and packed for inspection, who receives the report and who authorises release. Leave a supplier-agreed opportunity to correct issues. A rushed photo of cartons cannot answer every question about finish, quantity or matching parts. See inspection and factory visits for support.

Get these answers in writing

  • What are your last production, inspection and collection dates before closure?
  • When does the office reopen, when does production restart, and when do you expect normal output?
  • Which materials and components for our order are available, and which suppliers still need to deliver?
  • What is our confirmed production slot and what remains before the goods are ready?
  • Who is our holiday contact, and who can approve corrections or release goods?
  • What happens to our completion and collection dates if a component or inspection fails?

Keep one order tracker covering every supplier, product reference, quantity, approval status and promised milestone. Share changes with your designer, contractor and freight contact. For repeat units, check that a revised quantity or finish will not change production arrangements; our furniture MOQ guide helps frame that discussion.

If your order is already caught by the holiday

First establish what physically exists: finished and inspected goods, unfinished goods, or materials awaiting production. Ask for an item-level update rather than a single percentage for the whole order. Identify critical items with your site team: a missing kitchen component may block installation even when most furniture is ready.

  • Split shipments: compare releasing complete, approved items now with holding everything. Obtain separate packing lists and a revised delivery plan before agreeing the split.
  • Prioritise critical items: group parts needed for a complete installation together. Sending cabinets without essential matching fittings may not help the contractor.
  • Use air freight selectively: IK ENG offers air freight for urgent items, samples and spare parts. Ask whether the specific item is suitable and compare the full quoted cost before booking. Air transport cannot solve unfinished production.
  • Consolidate deliberately: bring ready goods from several suppliers together when this supports the programme. Agree whether to wait for a late supplier or release the ready group separately.

After restart, reconfirm the specification and inspection plan before accepting a new completion date. Ask which dependencies have changed and who is responsible for the next update. Tell the site team which dates are confirmed and which remain provisional so they can resequence work.

Frequently asked questions

Do all factories close for the same dates?

No. Ask each supplier for its own last production day, collection deadline, restart date and expected return to normal output in writing.

Does a factory reopening mean my order is ready?

No. Confirm which production stage your order has reached, whether components are available and when finished goods can be inspected and collected.

Can urgent items travel by air?

IK ENG offers air freight for urgent items, samples and spare parts. Ask for a shipment-specific assessment before splitting the order.

Should I skip inspection to catch the last shipment?

Keep the agreed inspection and correction steps. If they cannot be completed before collection, compare a revised shipment with a split order rather than accepting unchecked goods.
