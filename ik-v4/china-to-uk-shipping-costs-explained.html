Shipping cost China to UK: what every cost line means

A China-to-UK shipping quote should separate collection, export clearance, freight, destination handling, customs brokerage, duty, import VAT and delivery, with insurance if requested. Each shipment is quoted individually with every cost on its own line. Compare the same collection and delivery scope before comparing totals.

Origin collection

This is the move from the Chinese supplier to the agreed receiving point or departure facility. Distance, the number of pickup addresses, carton sizes, weight, loading access and equipment needs affect the work. Several suppliers can mean several collections; ask whether consolidation handling and any storage are separate. Confirm what the supplier terms already include.

Export clearance

This line covers the agreed work to prepare and process the export entry. The number of goods lines, document quality and any extra checks or permits can change the work involved. Ask who supplies the export information and who pays under the agreed supplier terms. Clear, consistent invoices and packing lists help avoid amendments.

Ocean or air freight

Main freight covers the booked international movement. The route, sailing or flight availability, shipment size and weight, packing and cargo requirements affect the quote. LCL shares container space; FCL reserves a container for your shipment. Air quotations take account of weight and the space occupied, so light but bulky cartons can matter.

Compare LCL versus FCL for your packed goods. Sea freight is about 5 weeks at sea; the full journey includes other stages. See China-to-UK shipping times. No freight price should be treated as a permanent tariff.

Destination port and handling charges

These cover agreed receiving, terminal or depot handling and, for LCL, separating the shared load. The arrival location, shipment type, cargo dimensions and handling requirements affect the charges. Ask what is included and when storage or other time-related charges could start. Late paperwork or an unavailable delivery slot can create extra work or storage.

Customs brokerage

Brokerage pays for the customs representative’s agreed declaration work; it is separate from taxes. The number and complexity of commodity lines, special procedures and corrections can affect the quote. Supply accurate descriptions, invoices, packing lists and importer details before arrival.

GOV.UK explains that you can appoint someone to handle customs and transport, and that the commodity code is required on the import declaration: GOV.UK: importing goods into the UK. IK ENG arranges UK clearance through an authorised UK customs broker.

UK duty

Duty is a tax line, not a freight or brokerage fee. The commodity classification, origin and customs value affect the amount due. Confirm the correct code and valuation with the broker rather than applying an assumed furniture rate to a mixed shipment. See GOV.UK: importing goods into the UK and our import duty and VAT guide for furniture.

UK import VAT

Import VAT is separate from duty. Its valuation starts with the customs value and includes applicable duty and relevant incidental expenses, including transport and insurance to the first UK destination and a further destination if known at importation. Changes to those inputs can change the VAT amount. The goods’ VAT treatment and any eligible accounting arrangement also matter. See GOV.UK: working out the import VAT value and GOV.UK: paying VAT on imports.

Ask who is the importer and how tax is accounted for. A DDP quote should still identify duty and import VAT separately; read DDP versus DAP for the contractual allocation.

Final UK delivery

Delivery is the move from the arrival facility to the agreed UK address. Postcode, distance, vehicle size, access restrictions, unloading equipment and appointment requirements affect the job. Tell the agent about narrow roads, stairs, site rules and who will receive and unload the goods. Agree the exact delivery scope in writing.

Optional cargo insurance

Cargo insurance can be arranged on request and should be a separate line. The goods, insured value, packing, journey and cover requested affect the quotation. Read the cover, exclusions, excess and claims requirements before accepting; a freight booking alone should not be assumed to provide the cover you need.

How to compare an itemised quotation

  • Use the same supplier collection point, goods list, packed dimensions and UK delivery address for each quote.
  • Check for overlap with the supplier’s shipping terms and identify who handles import clearance and taxes.
  • Ask which charges are confirmed or estimated, the quote validity and how variations will be approved.
  • List consolidation, inspection and insurance separately if requested; confirm exclusions and any possible storage charges.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in shipping cost from China to the UK?

Check collection, export clearance, main freight, destination handling, customs brokerage, duty, import VAT and final delivery. Insurance and other optional services should be shown separately if requested.

Why does a freight-only quote differ from a delivered quote?

A freight-only quote may cover just the main international movement. A delivered quote has a wider scope, so compare the collection point, destination, taxes, handling and exclusions line by line.

Are customs brokerage and import duty the same charge?

No. Brokerage pays for the customs representative’s agreed work. Duty is a separate tax determined by the goods and applicable customs rules.

Does IK ENG publish a fixed China-to-UK shipping price?

No. Every shipment is quoted individually with every cost on its own line. Send the goods details, supplier location, packed dimensions and weights, ready date and UK postcode.
