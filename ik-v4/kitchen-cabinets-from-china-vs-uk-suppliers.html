Kitchen cabinets from China vs UK suppliers

For UK landlords, HMO owners and developers, compare the same cabinet specification and the full installed cost. Allow time for checks and decide how replacements will reach site.

Discuss kitchen cabinet sourcing

Start with the project, not the country

Comparing kitchen cabinets from China vs UK suppliers is useful only when both quotes cover the same kitchen. A landlord replacing a damaged rental kitchen needs a different buying route from a developer repeating an approved layout across several properties. Write down your installation date, kitchen schedule, finish expectations and who will resolve problems on site before asking for prices.

China sourcing can suit a planned programme with time for samples, drawings and inspection. A UK supplier can be the better choice for a small one-off job, a very short deadline or a property where measurements are still changing. Neither location guarantees quality. The specification, supplier checks and practical replacement plan matter more than the catalogue photograph.

Compare carcasses and doors separately

Ask each supplier to name the carcass substrate, board thickness, back construction, shelf supports and edge treatment. Melamine-faced particleboard, MDF and plywood describe different materials, not a complete quality grade. Moisture-resistant board still needs appropriate detailing around sinks and exposed edges; do not treat the description as a promise that the cabinet is waterproof.

Door specifications need their own line. Record whether the door uses MDF, solid timber or another core, then describe the surface: laminate, painted finish, veneer or wrapped finish, for example. Ask how edges, corners and reverse faces are finished. Check colour and sheen against a physical reference rather than accepting a screen image as approval.

For an HMO kitchen, think about repeated cleaning, knocks and how easily a hinge or drawer can be adjusted. Specify hardware models, hinge opening, drawer runners, fixing methods and handle centres. Ask whether alternatives require written approval. Our kitchen cabinet specification checklist helps turn these details into a consistent enquiry.

Make UK appliances and worktops fit on paper

Give the cabinet supplier the actual appliance model references and installation drawings. Nominal appliance widths do not settle every clearance. Check oven ventilation, fridge door movement, dishwasher door fixing, service voids and plinth arrangements. Mark water, waste, sockets and extraction positions on the approved plan, with your fitter reviewing access for installation and maintenance.

Decide who supplies and templates the worktop. If a UK fabricator will template after fitting, agree cabinet levels, support requirements, overhangs, sink position and end panels beforehand. Include fillers, corner clearances, scribes and finished ends in both quotes. A drawing that fits wall to wall without installation tolerance can leave the fitter solving avoidable problems.

Treat any electrical accessories as a separate specification and evidence check. Request the relevant UKCA documentation for the proposed model where applicable, rather than relying on a cabinet supplier's general assurance. Check the product-specific requirements using GOV.UK: product conformity guidance. This guide focuses on delivery to Great Britain; confirm the applicable route separately for Northern Ireland.

Build a complete landed and installed cost

A factory product total and a UK delivered kitchen total are different starting points. Ask for every component, identify what is excluded and compare both options at the same endpoint: installed, checked and usable. Keep estimates visibly separate from confirmed quotations, and record the currency and payment assumptions.

Product: include carcasses, doors, hardware, internal fittings, panels, fillers and spare components. Add design, sourcing, samples and inspection where charged. Freight: include supplier collection, origin handling, export arrangements, sea freight, UK handling and any consolidation or packing work. Ask about cargo insurance; IK ENG offers it on request.

Duty: classification and customs value need checking against the actual goods. Use GOV.UK: Trade Tariff with the broker, rather than assuming all kitchen items share one code or rate. The kitchen cabinet commodity code guide explains the questions to raise. GOV.UK: transaction value for imported goods explains transaction-value adjustments, including relevant transport and insurance costs.

VAT: ask the broker to calculate import VAT on the correct basis. GOV.UK: import VAT valuation explains that the VAT value starts with customs value and adds duty and relevant incidental expenses. Our furniture import duty and VAT guide provides further context. Keep tax cash flow visible and ask your accountant how your own circumstances affect the budget.

Delivery: check the final postcode, access, unloading equipment, carrying distance and whether delivery ends at the kerb or elsewhere. Installation: price assembly, fitting, worktop coordination and waste removal separately. Remedial work: identify who pays for return visits, replacement shipping, local adjustments or a missing panel. An allowance should reflect your project risks, not an assumed saving.

Lead times and minimum orders need written answers

Build the programme backwards from the date the fitter needs a complete kitchen. Include survey, drawing revisions, sample approval, production, inspection, corrective work, freight, clearance and final delivery. Ask when each stage can start and which approval releases production. A promised factory completion date is not a site delivery date.

Our China to UK shipping times guide explains the separate stages. Avoid booking dependent trades around an unconfirmed arrival. For phased developments, agree whether kitchens will be delivered together or by phase and where each batch can safely wait.

Ask about minimum quantities for the exact range and finish. Standard components and bespoke doors may have different ordering conditions; mixing colours or changing dimensions can change the proposal. Do not assume a factory will repeat a small batch on the original terms. Compare the whole package with a UK supplier's stock availability, order flexibility and cancellation conditions.

Approve samples and inspect against the schedule

Request a representative door, a carcass material sample and the proposed hardware where practical. Label the approved samples and retain photographs and written references. Check edge quality, finish under the intended lighting and how the sample cleans. Read our guide to ordering samples from China before treating a small swatch as approval for a finished assembly.

Agree what inspection will cover before the order is placed: dimensions, quantities, finish, hardware, assembly checks and packaging against the signed specification. Decide which units or components must be assembled for checking and how defects will be recorded. Receiving sealed cartons is not the same as checking the cabinet inside. Resolve documented discrepancies before authorising shipment.

Plan after-sales support before paying

Ask who owns a defect report and what evidence they need: carton labels, photographs, measurements and the approved drawing revision. Put the remedy process in writing, including who pays for labour and transport. An offer to replace a door at the factory does not by itself cover getting it to your fitter or returning to site.

For repeated rental layouts, retain the finish reference, hardware model and component schedule. Discuss spare hinges, handles, drawer parts and selected panels with the supplier. Check whether later batches could vary in colour, whether the range may change and whether compatible replacements are available locally. Keep a record for each property so maintenance staff can identify the right part.

When to choose a UK supplier

Choose a UK supplier when a small order cannot justify the coordination, when a vacancy window is very short or when you need local stock and quick collection of missing parts. It can also suit a renovation where walls, services or the final layout will only be confirmed during the work. Verify stock and replacement promises rather than assuming every UK catalogue item is immediately available.

Consider China sourcing when the scope is settled, you can approve materials before production and repeated layouts make detailed specification worthwhile. Frequently asked questions

Are kitchen cabinets from China always cheaper?

No. Compare the same specification and include freight, duty, VAT, delivery, installation and remedial work before deciding.

Will cabinets from China fit UK appliances?

Check the actual appliance installation drawings against the cabinet drawings, including ventilation, service access and door clearances.

When is a UK kitchen supplier the better choice?

A UK supplier can be better for a small one-off job, a very short deadline or a changing layout, especially where local stock and replacement support matter.

What should I approve before production?

Approve the drawings, material and finish samples, hardware schedule, inspection scope and written replacement process before production.
