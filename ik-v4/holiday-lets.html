How it works for holiday let owners

  1. Set the guest inventory. Send plans, guest capacity, room list, reference images, budget, delivery postcode and required date. Mark retained items and separate operational purchases.
  2. Review everyday use. Check sofa-bed use, dining capacity, luggage and bedding storage, family use and cleaning access. Request care instructions and details of replaceable parts.
  3. Approve samples and scope. Agree finishes, dimensions, product evidence and any fitted-item drawings. List optional outdoor or operational packages separately before approving the quotation.
  4. Inspect before shipping. Check the finished order against approved references and receive the pre-shipment photo report. Keep product codes and finish samples for future replacement enquiries.
  5. Prepare for the first booking. Agree receiving, unloading and delivery access. Allow for assembly, checking, cleaning and photography after delivery before guests arrive.

Holiday let owners FAQs

Can you source furniture for a single holiday let?

Yes. There is no minimum to enquire. Supplier quantities and shipping practicality are checked against the actual products and room schedule.

Does the furniture order include outdoor and guest equipment?

Outdoor furniture, hot tubs, appliances and operational guest equipment are separate packages with their own specifications and approvals. List them explicitly rather than assuming they are part of the core furniture order.

Which samples should I approve for a holiday let?

Choose samples that resolve the main decisions: finish samples for colour and surface, construction samples for joinery, or a full product where comfort, size or assembly matters. Record approved references for production and inspection.

How should I plan replacements between bookings?

Keep product references, finish samples, care instructions and agreed spare-part details. Check urgent replacements separately rather than assuming a new made-to-order item can arrive during a changeover.
