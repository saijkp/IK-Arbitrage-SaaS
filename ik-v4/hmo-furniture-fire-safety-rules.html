HMO Furniture Fire Safety Rules: What Landlords Must Know

Keep furniture checks alongside your room inventory. Identify what you are buying, agree the right specification and retain evidence for the actual items delivered. Labels, mattress documents and property fire arrangements each answer different questions.

Know what the furniture rules cover

The Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988, as amended, set fire-performance requirements for domestic upholstered furniture. Landlords supplying furniture need to ensure it is fire safe. Start with the current GOV.UK landlord safety guidance and the Trading Standards furniture guidance.

For a purchasing schedule, separate upholstered furniture from plain timber or metal items and list mattresses and bed bases explicitly. A room pack can contain several product types with different evidence. One general statement on a quotation does not tell you which sofa, mattress or upholstered headboard was checked.

Permanent labels and swing labels are different

The 2025 amendments removed the display or swing-label requirement from 30 October 2025. They did not remove the permanent-label obligations. The official amendment guidance explains the change.

Check the permanent label on relevant upholstered furniture and photograph it while the item is easy to access. Record the room and product reference beside the photograph. Do not cut it off when unpacking or assembling. Keep packaging information until you have matched every item to the delivery schedule.

Mattresses and BS 7177

Mattresses, divans and bed bases have a different labelling position from ordinary upholstered seating. BS 7177 covers ignition resistance for mattresses, mattress pads, divans and bed bases. Do not treat a sofa's permanent label and a mattress's BS 7177 evidence as interchangeable.

IK ENG specifies BS 7177 for mattresses and beds. Ask for the exact product reference and the performance evidence relevant to its intended use. A plain bed frame, an upholstered headboard and a mattress are different components: list them separately so no one assumes that a document for one automatically covers the others.

Before purchasing for an HMO, share the intended use and any requirements from your property fire assessment or licence with the supplier. Keep the selected specification with the order. Avoid selecting a product from a photograph first and trying to reconstruct its materials and test evidence after manufacture.

Build a clear property file

A practical file connects each room item to its purchase and supporting documents. Use consistent names throughout: bedroom three, bed frame B03, mattress M03. Save a copy of the approved schedule and later revisions so a substitute product does not disappear into a generic description such as “double bed”.

  • Keep the supplier's details, quotation, invoice and delivery date.
  • Save product references, dimensions, materials and agreed finishes.
  • File label photographs and available test certificates against those references.
  • Record substitutions and the evidence received for replacement items.
  • Keep assembly and care instructions with the room inventory.

This is a suggested filing method, rather than a claim that every landlord must use a particular folder format. A spreadsheet, labelled digital folders or a property-management system can all work. Choose a method that lets you find a product's records when the room is occupied and the packaging has gone.

Check the actual delivery

Have the approved schedule available when goods arrive. Count cartons, record visible damage and check product references before distributing everything around the house. Photograph accessible labels and set aside any unexpected item until the supplier explains the difference. Do not assume that a similar colour means the same product has arrived.

For a larger delivery, nominate one person to update the inventory. If several installers are unpacking in parallel, give them room references and a simple way to flag discrepancies. This saves time later when a mattress is in the wrong room or a product document cannot be matched to an item.

Imported furniture needs traceable evidence

When buying from China, ask for evidence for the selected model and materials rather than a factory's general marketing certificate. The importer and supplier need to understand their responsibilities. Useful questions include who supplies the product, which model the report identifies and whether a material change requires fresh checks.

IK ENG brings product specifications and test certificates together in a compliance pack. Keep that pack with your approved order and delivery records. It should remain possible to identify the actual furniture after the shipment has been split between bedrooms or multiple properties. Our inspection service can be planned around the agreed order specification.

Second-hand and replacement furniture

Furniture bought between tenancies deserves the same organised purchasing process as the original fit-out. Record the source, product and condition, and check the relevant evidence before adding it to the room. A low price can become inconvenient if no one can identify what was supplied.

Do not buy a loose label to solve an evidence gap. Ask the original supplier to identify the product and explain its documentation. If you cannot establish what the item is, hold it out of the furnishing plan while you resolve the question. This is a practical purchasing control, not a substitute for advice about a particular item.

Review furniture between tenancies

Use the check-out and pre-let inspections to compare the room against its inventory. Look for missing items, loose fixings, torn upholstery, altered covers and labels that are no longer readable. Record repairs and replacements, with dated photographs where helpful, rather than simply carrying the old inventory forward.

Ask the supplier about compatible replacement parts and approved care methods. Recovering or modifying upholstered furniture can change the product you originally bought. Keep a record of the proposed work and resolve any effect on the original specification before returning the item to use.

Furniture checks are part of the wider property plan

A furniture compliance pack does not replace the property's fire-risk assessment, escape arrangements, alarm provision or HMO licence conditions. Coordinate your purchasing with the person responsible for those matters. Requirements may differ between properties, so give the supplier the actual brief rather than assuming that another landlord's pack will suit yours.

For an order enquiry, send room quantities, bed sizes, upholstered items and the delivery date, together with any property-specific requirements already identified.
