Chinese kitchen cabinets: UK appliance sizes

Use the actual appliance models and manufacturer installation drawings to approve cabinet openings, furniture doors, ventilation and services before production.

Plan your kitchen cabinet order

Why appliance fit needs its own drawing check

When specifying Chinese kitchen cabinets for UK appliance sizes, start with the appliances you will actually install. Chinese factories can make cabinets to any size, but default ranges may follow other markets. A catalogue description such as oven housing or integrated fridge cabinet does not confirm the usable opening, door fixing or ventilation arrangement.

Commonly, appliances are described as 600 mm wide; integrated dishwashers are commonly sold in 600 mm formats and slimline dishwashers commonly in 450 mm formats. These are planning descriptions, not manufacturing instructions. Always follow the specific appliance's installation instructions. External appliance width, required niche width and cabinet outside width are different measurements.

A cabinet can look right on an elevation and still obstruct a door, hose or air path. Resolve those interfaces before approving production. Start with our kitchen cabinet specification guide, then use this drawing pack to coordinate the factory, kitchen designer and installer.

Send actual model drawings, not just model names

Choose the exact appliance models and obtain the manufacturer's installation drawings for each one. Send the full relevant instructions, including notes around the diagrams. A retail product image or headline dimensions usually omit fixing points, clearances and service requirements. Record the full model reference, document revision and any accessory kits required for the chosen installation.

Make an appliance schedule linking each model to a cabinet number on the plan. Mark dimensions in millimetres and distinguish finished openings from overall cabinet sizes. Ask the factory to return coordinated plans, elevations and sections showing how each manufacturer's requirements are met. If instructions appear to conflict with the design, ask the manufacturer or installer to resolve the issue before the drawing is signed.

Lock the appliance schedule with the approved cabinet drawings. Substituting a model later can affect hinges, ventilation and cut-outs even if its advertised size is similar. Treat any substitution as a design change that needs another check, and tell the factory which previous drawing it replaces.

Built-in ovens and microwaves

For a built-in oven, show the finished housing opening, support shelf position, appliance fixing points and the ventilation path required by its instructions. Check that the cabinet construction can provide the specified support. Locate the electrical connection so it does not reduce the required space or become inaccessible once the oven is fitted.

Microwaves need the same model-specific treatment. Confirm whether the selected product is intended for built-in use and whether it needs a particular trim or installation kit. Do not put a freestanding model into a closed housing simply because its outside dimensions fit. For stacked ovens and microwaves, draw both openings and the intervening construction, with the ventilation and service requirements of each product.

Integrated dishwashers and fridge-freezers

For an integrated dishwasher, check the required opening, available height and adjustment range against the finished floor and underside of the worktop. Show the furniture door dimensions and fixing arrangement. Draw the door in its open position so the installer can check whether it clears the plinth. Place water, waste and electrical services as the instructions require, allowing access for future maintenance.

Integrated fridge-freezers require a clear decision about door-on-door or sliding hinge installation. The furniture front and appliance door connect differently, so the hinge system cannot be left as a generic note. Send the exact manufacturer's fitting diagram and identify the furniture door split, door weights where specified, fixing positions and opening direction.

Draw the full ventilation route for refrigeration, including the intake and outlet required by the selected model. A decorative grille does not prove that the route behind it is clear. Check nearby walls, handles and adjacent doors so the appliance opens far enough for its intended use and maintenance. Ask the factory to show these relationships on a section and plan.

Hobs, extractors, sinks and taps

Hob cut-outs must follow the specific installation drawing, including corner details, worktop suitability and required clearances. Show the space below the hob and any restrictions affecting drawers, dividers or an oven underneath. Do not cut the worktop from the appliance's overall glass dimensions. Agree whether the cut-out will be made in the factory or checked and cut on site.

For the extractor, send its mounting drawing and identify whether the design uses ducting or recirculation. Coordinate cabinet depth, fixings, service access and any duct route with the installer. Follow the manufacturer's requirements for the relationship between the extractor and the selected hob; do not substitute a general clearance from another product.

For sinks and taps, include actual templates, bowl depth, mounting method, tap hole positions and clearances. Check that the bowl, clips, waste and trap can fit inside the sink cabinet without colliding with drawers or shelves. Mark which holes are factory-made and which will be confirmed on site. A sink template should be matched to the final product before cutting.

Service voids, plinths and worktop geometry

Show service voids in section, with pipes, hoses, sockets and isolation points coordinated by the installer. A cabinet's advertised depth may include space that the appliance cannot occupy. Check the usable depth after backs, rails and services are allowed for, and retain access to connections that need maintenance.

Set plinth heights from the finished floor level and coordinate them with appliance feet and furniture door movement. Include any floor finish that has yet to be laid. A drawing measured from the unfinished floor can change the appliance opening once tiles or other flooring are added.

Specify worktop depth, thickness and overhang together with the cabinet fronts and handles. Check that the front projection does not interfere with appliance controls or door movement. Show end panels and fillers on the plan, including the space needed at walls, corners and tall units. These pieces help accommodate site conditions, but cannot rescue an opening that was designed around the wrong appliance.

Electrical items and installation planning

For electrical items included in your sourcing brief, specify UKCA marking and request supporting conformity documentation for the exact model. Do not treat a mark on a catalogue image as evidence for the delivered product. The GOV.UK electrical equipment guidance for Great Britain explains the relevant safety framework and business responsibilities. Keep electrical product checks separate from cabinet dimensional approval.

Use a qualified electrician to plan electrical connections and assess installation requirements. In England, discuss Part P with them using GOV.UK Approved Document P. This guidance concerns electrical safety in dwellings; it is not a cabinet sizing standard. Confirm the applicable arrangements for the property's location before work starts.

Approve, sample and check before dispatch

Ask the installer to review the coordinated drawing pack before the factory starts production. Frequently asked questions

Can Chinese factories make cabinets for UK appliances?

Yes. Chinese factories can make cabinets to any size, but default ranges may follow other markets. Approve drawings against the exact appliances you will install.

Is a 600 mm appliance description enough to order a cabinet?

No. Always follow the specific appliance's installation instructions for the opening, services, ventilation and fixing requirements.

Which fridge-freezer hinge detail should I send?

Send the exact model installation drawing and confirm whether the appliance uses door-on-door or sliding hinge installation, together with the furniture door split and ventilation route.
