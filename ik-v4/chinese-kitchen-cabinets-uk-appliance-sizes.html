Chinese kitchen cabinets: UK appliance sizes

The right fit starts with the actual appliance models. Send their manufacturer installation drawings and coordinate openings, doors, services and finishing panels before production.

Explore kitchen cabinet sourcing

Why appliance fit needs its own approval

Chinese factories can make cabinets to any size, but their default ranges may follow other markets. A familiar-looking kitchen drawing does not prove that your chosen UK oven, dishwasher or fridge-freezer will fit. Agree the appliances and installation requirements before the factory releases cabinet sizes for production.

Appliances are commonly described as 600 mm wide, with integrated dishwashers commonly sold for 600 mm spaces and slimline models commonly described as 450 mm. These are shopping descriptions, not manufacturing dimensions. Always follow the specific appliance's installation instructions. The body, opening, door arrangement and space behind it can differ.

Start with our kitchen cabinet checks guide, then make an appliance schedule. The factory should be able to identify the exact model and drawing used for every opening. Avoid approving a drawing marked only with a generic appliance name.

Send the actual models' manufacturer installation drawings

Choose full model references, including suffixes, and download the manufacturer installation instructions. Supply complete PDFs with readable dimensions and all relevant pages. A retailer photograph or headline width does not describe the installation space. Include model references and document revisions in the pack.

Ask the factory to confirm that it has reviewed those documents against its cabinet drawings. The installer should review them too, especially where services or ventilation depend on the room. If an appliance changes, reopen approval for every affected cabinet, panel and worktop cut-out. A replacement in the same nominal size may have different requirements.

Built-in oven and microwave housings

For each oven and microwave, show the housing opening, supporting shelf position and ventilation route. Include external front dimensions as well as the required space behind. Check the door movement and handle position against neighbouring doors, drawers and any wall return.

With stacked appliances, draw the whole tower in elevation and section. Show shelf thicknesses, manufacturer-required separation, microwave position and connection access. Confirm support construction with the factory and installer. Do not copy the oven's overall height straight into a cabinet opening without checking its installation drawing.

Integrated dishwasher: door and plinth movement

An integrated dishwasher needs the correct opening and a compatible furniture front. Send the model's installation diagram, mounting template and stated door size and weight requirements. Show the front panel height, fixing positions and handle. Ask the factory to identify the appliance's door mechanism and explain how it works with the cabinet design.

Check the full opening movement against the plinth and adjacent panels. Show any required plinth recess or cut-out. The Bosch dishwasher installation guide stresses that measurements and methods vary by model. Use your own model's instructions for connections and clearances, even if an example looks similar.

Fridge-freezer housings and hinge systems

Specify whether the integrated fridge-freezer uses door-on-door fixing or a sliding hinge system. Send the manufacturer's drawings for that exact system, including furniture door requirements. Show the split between fridge and freezer doors; a different split can make an otherwise attractive cabinet front unusable.

Mark opening direction, hinge side, clearance beside walls and the space needed to open doors and remove drawers. Show the ventilation inlet, path through the housing and outlet as instructed by the manufacturer. Check that decorative panels, fillers and back panels leave the route clear. Ask the installer to consider future appliance removal too.

Hob cut-outs and extractor position

The hob's outside dimensions are not its cut-out dimensions. Supply the manufacturer cut-out drawing with corner details, worktop requirements, edge distances and clearances below and beside it. Show how it relates to drawers or an oven underneath. Tie its centreline to the kitchen plan so cabinet and worktop drawings agree.

For an extractor, send installation instructions and show its position relative to the hob. Agree the required separation, fixing arrangement and duct route or specified recirculation arrangement. Check for conflicts with shelving, tall units and services. Resolve these details with the installer before cutting a cabinet or worktop.

Service voids and ventilation gaps

Draw sections through relevant units. Show water, waste, sockets, isolators and ducts relative to the appliance envelope. Keep connections accessible as required by the installation instructions. A pipe or plug behind an appliance can consume depth that the catalogue drawing appeared to leave available.

Distinguish service voids from ventilation paths: they serve different purposes and should not be guessed from a standard cabinet depth. Mark clear space remaining after back panels, brackets and services are included. Ask the factory to dimension it and the installer to confirm planned connection positions before manufacture.

Plinths, worktops, sinks and finishing pieces

Agree the finished floor reference, adjustable leg arrangement, plinth height and worktop level together. Show worktop thickness, depth and front overhang in section, measured against the finished door face. Check doors and drawers against handles and projecting edges. Include changes of floor level or finish affecting the run.

For the sink, supply the actual manufacturer template and fitting method. Mark the bowl, clips, waste and tap against cabinet internals. Confirm tap hole positions, hole numbers and clearance for the handle and spout. Agree whether final holes or cut-outs should follow a site check rather than an unverified plan.

Include end panels and fillers in the schedule with finished thicknesses and locations. Allow for wall irregularities and check corner doors, handles, appliance doors and nearby room doors through their movements. A filler is part of the fitting plan, not an unexplained leftover dimension. Label loose pieces for the installer.

Approve one coordinated version

Number cabinet units consistently across the plan, elevations, sections and packing schedule. Record questions on the drawing and obtain a revised version incorporating answers. A message saying that an opening will be adjusted is easy to lose when production staff work from another PDF.

Use samples to assess finishes and construction, while checking dimensions through approved drawings. Before dispatch, compare critical openings, front panels and labelled parts against the final revision. Keep copies for the fitter so receiving checks and assembly use the same specification.

Give the installer a final review point before authorising production. Ask them to flag missing dimensions, service access problems and any site measurements needing confirmation. Record who approved the appliance layout and the cabinet drawings, with dates. If the factory proposes a substitution, check its effect on the whole pack before accepting it.

Electrical items and sourcing support

If electrical items are included, request UKCA marking and supporting product documentation as part of your purchasing specification. Review applicable product requirements using GOV.UK's UKCA guidance. Cabinet dimensions do not establish electrical compliance; get suitable installation advice for the selected appliances.

Frequently asked questions

Will Chinese kitchen cabinets fit UK appliances?

They can be made to suit your selected appliances. Confirm openings and clearances against the specific models' manufacturer installation instructions before production.

Is a nominal 600 mm appliance size enough for drawings?

No. A nominal size is only a starting description. Always follow the specific appliance's installation instructions for the opening, ventilation, services and door movement.

What should the factory receive before making the cabinets?

Send a measured room plan, full appliance model references and manufacturer installation drawings, plus coordinated cabinet plans, elevations and sections for approval.
