Hotel Room Refurbishment Furniture Checklist

Start a hotel room refurbishment with a measured room schedule and an approved specification for each room type. Work back from reopening through installation, delivery, inspection, production and sampling, allowing for rooms that remain occupied.

Survey every room and build the schedule

Record dimensions, door swings, sockets, radiators, skirtings and fixed services. Photograph access routes and identify unusual rooms instead of treating the floor plan as a complete survey. Give every room a reference and list what stays, what is replaced and how many of each item it needs.

Write a specification sheet per room type

List beds, headboards, bedside tables, wardrobes, desks, luggage racks, seating, mirrors and lighting. Record dimensions, materials, finishes, hardware and cleaning instructions. Attach drawings, finish references and a revision date so factory, inspector and installer use the same approved version.

Approve a sample or mock-up room

Check dimensions, bed clearance, wardrobe operation, lighting positions and luggage space in the proposed layout. Compare finishes in the room’s light and test cleaning on samples. Record changes before signing off production; a photograph alone cannot confirm fit or finish.

Agree fire performance and durability

Ask the operator and fire safety adviser to define the upholstery and mattress specification. Request evidence for the actual materials and combinations ordered, alongside expected labels. Specify robust edges, runners, hinges and surfaces for heavy guest use. Read fire safety for hotel and holiday let furniture and have the evidence reviewed before approval.

Review electrics integrated into furniture

Mark lighting, sockets and USB units on drawings. Ask for the exact model documentation, wiring diagrams and English manuals, and let the electrician review access, connections and installation. IK ENG checks that documentation is supplied; it does not certify products.

Order spares with the main batch

List spare fronts, handles, hinges and fabric, with item references and a storage location. Keep approved finish samples and the final specification for later orders. Confirm future availability rather than assuming an identical finish will remain in production.

Work back from reopening

Reserve time for installation, snagging and cleaning after delivery. Standard items typically take 10–12 weeks to site; bespoke items 12–18 weeks from approved drawings. Build survey and sampling time before that order period, and confirm production, inspection, shipping, customs and final-delivery milestones within the quoted programme. Allow for delays and do not treat the production date as the arrival date.

Phase work by floor or room block

Map closures, occupied rooms and safe access with the operator. Give each delivery a floor and room list, and agree where crews can work without blocking guest routes. Confirm whether phased production or local storage suits the reopening programme.

Check delivery access and storage

Measure vehicle approach, unloading areas, doorways, lifts and stairs using packed dimensions. Agree offloading equipment, delivery booking and a dry, secure storage area. Check cartons against the packing list before moving them to the labelled rooms.

Arrange disposal of old furniture

Schedule removal, reuse or appropriate disposal before new stock arrives. Confirm who dismantles and removes old items, and keep waste routes separate from guest and new-furniture routes. Include this work in the closure programme.

Turn the checklist into a brief

Frequently asked questions

Should every room have its own survey?

Record each room and check differences in dimensions, services and access. Group rooms into types only after those differences are understood.

Is a mock-up room useful?

Yes. It lets the operator check fit, finishes, circulation and furniture operation before approving the repeated specification.

Do the lead times include sampling?

Plan survey and sampling before the quoted order period. Confirm each production, inspection, shipping and delivery milestone and allow time for installation afterwards.

What spares should we order?

Consider fronts, handles, hinges and fabric for the agreed furniture, recording part references and storage arrangements with the final specification.
