How to Furnish a Serviced Apartment from China: Room by Room

A serviced apartment has to work every day for guests who did not choose the furniture and will not look after it like their own. Plan it room by room, with one specification you can repeat across every unit.

Start with the unit schedule, not the shopping list

Before you look at products, write down what each apartment type needs: number of bedrooms, sleeping capacity, whether there is a kitchen and a work area, and how many units share the same layout. Most serviced apartment blocks have only a few unit types. Specify each type once and multiply it.

Add measured floor plans, door and lift sizes, and the delivery postcode. Our serviced apartment furnishing service starts from this kind of brief. Factory options and pricing come back within 48 hours of a complete brief, so the clearer the schedule, the faster you can compare.

Bedrooms

The bed and mattress carry most of the guest experience, and most of the compliance risk. Order beds and mattresses that meet BS 7177, and ask for the evidence in writing. Choose a sturdy base with a headboard that is easy to wipe and hard to damage.

  • Beds and mattresses: confirm size, base height and mattress depth together so linen fits and the bed is easy to make.
  • Bedside tables or wall shelves: allow a charging point within reach.
  • Wardrobe or hanging space: guests staying a week or more need real storage. For fitted units, see fitted wardrobes and joinery.
  • Chair or bench: somewhere to sit that is not the bed.

Living and dining areas

Upholstered furniture needs the most care. Sofas, armchairs and upholstered dining chairs are covered by fire safety rules for furniture supplied in the UK, and relevant pieces should carry permanent fire labels. Check the current requirements in the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 and confirm how they apply to each piece before you order.

Choose fabrics that clean easily and hold their shape. A sofa bed can add sleeping capacity, but check how the mechanism will cope with repeated use by different guests.

  • A sofa sized for the room as measured, with the door and lift route checked.
  • A dining table and chairs matched to the number of guests the unit sleeps.
  • A TV unit or wall mount point agreed with whoever installs the screens.
  • A coffee or side table with a wipeable top.

Kitchen and kitchenette

Guests cook, so the kitchen needs more than a kettle. Decide early whether you want full cabinets and worktops or a compact kitchenette. Send appliance sizes with the brief so cabinets are drawn around them. Our guide to kitchen cabinets from China covers the points to check on materials, edging and hinges.

Include crockery, glasses, cutlery, pans and storage in the schedule, with quantities per unit. These small items are easy to forget and slow to buy one apartment at a time.

Bathrooms

Keep bathroom fittings simple and consistent. Agree vanity units, mirrors, shelves and accessories in the same schedule as the furniture so everything arrives together. If you are also sourcing fittings, see our bathroom sourcing page. Check that anything fixed to walls suits your wall construction and that fittings can be replaced with the same item later.

Lighting and electrical goods

Lamps, LED fittings and other electrical goods need UKCA conformity documents. Ask for these before you order, not after the goods arrive. Plan plugs, switches and charging points with your electrician so the lighting layout and furniture positions agree. Our LED lighting sourcing page explains how we handle lighting orders.

Work area, hallway and finishing touches

Many guests work remotely. A small desk, a comfortable chair and good light make a unit more useful than extra decoration. In the hallway, add a mirror, hooks and a place for luggage. Curtains or blinds, rugs, bins and a few wall items complete the unit.

Ask your designer or supplier for a mood board before ordering decorative items. Our in-house designer prepares layouts, mood boards and product schedules for a fixed fee agreed in advance, credited against the order. See design and specification for what is included.

Specify for durability and repeat orders

A serviced apartment is used hard and turned around quickly. Favour solid construction, hard-wearing finishes and parts you can reorder. Record every product, finish and size in one schedule so a replacement chair next year matches the original. If you manage several buildings, the same schedule can be reused, as explained in our guide to furnishing multiple properties.

Samples, drawings and approvals

Approve finishes and key products before production. For bespoke items such as kitchens and fitted storage, work from approved drawings and name one person who signs them off. Late changes to dimensions can cause delays. Our shipping times guide shows how approvals feed into the overall programme.

Budget, payment and timing

Our sourcing fee is 5% of product value and appears on every quotation. Pay a 50% deposit to place the order, 30% after factory video confirmation of your finished goods, and 20% on delivery. Standard items typically take 10–12 weeks to site. Bespoke items typically take 12–18 weeks from approved drawings. Duty and VAT depend on the commodity code of each product, so confirm them before you order using our import duty and VAT guide.

A short checklist before you order

  1. One schedule per unit type, with quantities and sizes.
  2. Measured plans, access routes and the delivery postcode.
  3. BS 7177 evidence for mattresses and beds.
  4. Permanent fire labels on relevant upholstered pieces.
  5. UKCA documents for electrical goods.
  6. Approved drawings for kitchens and fitted joinery.
  7. A named person to approve changes.
