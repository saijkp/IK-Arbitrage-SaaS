How it works for property developers

  1. Set the show-home brief. Send plans, buyer profile, finish requirements and any presentation upgrades. Separate display choices from the specification intended for occupied units.
  2. Schedule each unit type. Record dimensions and quantities for studios, apartments or houses, with exceptions and optional items. Keep furniture, kitchens, bathrooms and lighting identifiable.
  3. Approve before repeating. Review samples or a sample unit, then sign off product references, drawings, finishes and permitted alternatives before production.
  4. Group orders by handover. Agree block, floor or plot allocations, factory readiness, packing references and any storage needed between production and site availability.
  5. Inspect and deliver in phases. Check goods against the approved revision and coordinate shipping and delivery around the agreed handovers. Review milestones when the build programme changes.

Keep show-home upgrades visible

A show-home selection can share a palette with buyer or rental packs while using different pieces. Keep those upgrades as named alternatives rather than allowing the display selection to become an unexplained allowance for every unit. Control drawing revisions and substitutions centrally so purchasing, inspection and the site team use the same approved references.

Property developers FAQs

Can show-home furniture differ from the repeat-unit pack?

Yes. Record show-home upgrades as named alternatives. Approve the repeated unit specification separately, including dimensions, finishes and product references, so the display selection does not define every pack by assumption.

Can one furniture specification cover different unit types?

Use common finish references with a separate dimension and quantity schedule for each layout. Check furniture fit, storage and circulation in each unit type before approving repeat production.

How are phased developer deliveries planned?

Group the schedule by block, floor or handover date. Agree plot labels, access, receiving contacts and storage, then coordinate production and shipping against those stages. Review milestones when the build programme changes.

What should a developer send for a furniture quotation?

Send plans, the unit mix, show-home requirements, finish levels, quantities, delivery postcode, budget and handover stages. Identify installation and specialist delivery requirements separately.
