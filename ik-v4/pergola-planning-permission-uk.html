Pergola Planning Permission in the UK

A garden pergola may fall within permitted development in England, depending on its design, position and the property. Check your proposal with the council before ordering; the householder route is not a general permission for every pergola or commercial site.

Start with England’s householder guidance

Use the GOV.UK householder technical guidance to discuss the design with your council. Class E is a possible starting point for a freestanding garden structure; an attached roof or verandah may be treated differently. Check the whole proposal, including coverage, use and any removed permitted development rights.

Height, boundaries and position

Class E guidance includes a 2.5 m overall limit where any part is within 2 m of a boundary. Elsewhere, roof form and eaves limits matter. Land forward of the original house’s principal elevation is excluded from that route. Ask the council how these limits apply to your drawings and ground levels.

Raised platforms and decking

The guidance excludes raised platforms over 0.3 m under Class E. Assess decking and the pergola together; low decking alone does not establish permission for the structure above it. Check levels with the council.

Listed buildings, conservation areas and flats

Listed-building surroundings and conservation areas have extra restrictions. Flats do not share these householder rights. Check property status, planning conditions and local directions with the council before relying on permitted development.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland

The linked technical guidance applies to England. Ask the relevant council about the rules for your UK nation and site; do not transfer England’s limits to another jurisdiction.

Pubs, restaurants, hotels and other commercial sites

Commercial pergolas are more likely to need a planning application; householder rights do not establish permission for a hospitality terrace. Discuss the structure, screens, lighting and intended use with the council early, alongside any other consents.

Ask about a Lawful Development Certificate

Consider asking the council about a Lawful Development Certificate for the proposed design before committing. Keep its drawings consistent with the factory order. Confirm planning and other consents separately.

Frequently asked questions

Is every garden pergola permitted development?

No. Design, position, property type and restrictions affect the route. Ask your council to assess the proposed drawings before ordering.

Can I use England’s householder guidance for a pub?

It does not establish permission for a commercial site. Discuss the proposed hospitality installation with the council.

Does the same guidance apply across the UK?

The linked householder technical guidance applies to England. Check the relevant rules with your council in Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland.

Can I obtain written confirmation before buying?

Ask the council whether a Lawful Development Certificate is appropriate for the proposal and what drawings and evidence it needs.
